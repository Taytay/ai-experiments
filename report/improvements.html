1. What improved, in order of impact

  1. Diverse text instead of a database dump. One sentence per entity, repeated 56 times, is memorized but barely usable (21.7% category inference). The same facts as 14 paraphrases and question-answer pairs double it (41.7%) and go from chance to 42.5% on reverse lookup. This is the single largest lever and it costs nothing at inference.
  2. Learning rate. Full fine-tuning at 5e-5 destroyed a 0.5B model (perplexity 16 to 800) and with it the products-to-category reasoning the task needs. At 1e-5 the same data gives the best result with perplexity 26. For LoRA, 2e-4 on rank 64 was also too hot; 1e-4 halved the damage with identical recall.
  3. No new tokens. Adding entity names as vocabulary collapsed the embedding model's transfer to statement-format strings from 70.8% to 23% and cut LLM extraction from 46.7% to 31.7%. Subword pieces are what let a learned association survive a format change, and they are the only route to your "slight variation on a name still means something" goal (section 4).
  4. Prototypes for user-defined labels. Defining a label as the centroid of the user's own examples handles nonsense names, synonyms and latent groupings with no retraining: 85% on type and 63% on habitat from three cards, against 33% chance.
  5. Model scale for few-shot label induction. The Timmy prompt is solvable by a 3B model with facts in context (49 to 52%) and not by a 0.5B model under any condition. Below a few billion parameters the model cannot override its label priors from three examples.
  6. Pipeline. Unsloth's fused kernels and loss give 2.2x throughput and 3x memory reduction at 512 tokens; sequence packing matters as much on small models, which are launch-overhead-bound at 13 to 17% GPU utilization on short facts. A 6-attribute entity costs about 1,800 training tokens for 100% recall, so 10,000 entities train in 17 minutes on 0.5B or 2 to 4 hours on 3B to 7B.

2. Your goals, restated against the data

You are fine with general perplexity rising, want to keep enough of the base model to bootstrap the target tasks, and want the result to be better at few-shot classification on unseen categories and inputs. Three of the findings bear directly on that.

The one kind of forgetting you cannot afford is loss of in-context learning. Every LoRA run on declarative text recalled its facts perfectly (100% in the trained format) and every one of them got worse at the few-shot label task when the facts were then supplied in context: 3B fell from 48.8 to 34.4 after training on declarative text, and on public few-shot data from 60 to 51. Perplexity on English prose is the wrong regression metric for you. The right one is a small suite of few-shot tasks with arbitrary labels, run before and after every training job; section 5 builds that suite and shows the data mix that reverses the loss.

Second, knowledge injected as statements did not become knowledge the model could use for analogy. Recall and manipulation are separate skills (Physics of Language Models 3.2), and our ladder shows the split: yes/no and comparison formats that appeared in training scored 85 to 94%, the never-trained Timmy format sat at chance. Third, the embedding side already does what you want for unseen categories: a label is a centroid of examples, so a synonym, a nonsense tag, or a grouping the label never names all work without retraining.

3. Inject first and then teach the task, or teach the task as the injection?

Recommendation: one interleaved curriculum, not two stages (tested in section 5)

Mix three kinds of examples in every batch of a single training run:

  1. Knowledge text about the entities, in many surface forms and both directions (the augmented recipe that already works), including statement-style noisy variants of names.
  2. Few-shot episodes built from the same database: "A is 'zorp', B is 'quix', C is 'zorp'. D is ___", with labels drawn fresh and at random each episode, the grouping attribute varied (type, habitat, price band, region), the option set sometimes given and sometimes not, and a slice of entities and attributes held out so the task itself is tested on unseen categories. This is symbol tuning (Wei et al. 2023) applied to your own data, and it is the mechanism that teaches the model to override its label priors from examples.
  3. A small replay slice (10 to 20%) of generic few-shot tasks with random labels, so the in-context skill is preserved rather than merely not attacked.

Why not sequential. Stage one (knowledge) measurably damages the skill that stage two needs, and stage two then overwrites stage one: two rounds of forgetting with nothing holding either in place. The literature is consistent on this: Physics of LMs 3.1 finds knowledge is only extractable when instruction-style data is mixed into pretraining rather than added afterwards; Instruction Pre-Training and Self-Tuning are the same idea at scale; MetaICL and symbol tuning show the few-shot skill is itself trainable and transfers to unseen tasks and labels.

Why not episodes alone. An episode teaches "these two share a group" without much exposure to why, and knowledge needs many diverse exposures to become robust. The mix gives the model the facts and the habit of using them. Expect the episodes to be the smaller share of tokens (a quarter to a third) but the larger share of the effect on your target metric.

In practice, with unsloth on this card: build the mixed dataset as packed 512-token sequences, train full or high-rank LoRA at 1e-4 or below since you accept general forgetting, keep the held-out attribute and held-out entity splits sacred, and let the harness compare "knowledge only", "episodes only", "interleaved", and "sequential" on the same ladder. Section 5 reports exactly that sweep, run on 3B in one evening.

4. Getting meaning from a name variant, a drug stem, or a molecule

This is a question about what the tokenizer's subword pieces have been taught. The mechanism already exists and the merchant result is the proof: fine-tuning with the stock tokenizer let "Elrholm" learned from clean sentences be recognized inside "DEBIT CARD PURCHASE ELRHOLM STORE 4970" at 70.8%, because the pieces el ##rh ##ol ##m are shared. Whole-word tokens broke that link. Held-out species stayed at chance for the opposite reason: their names carried no shared signal at all, so there was nothing to generalize from.

So the design goal is to make sure the signal is in the pieces, and to train on the variation you want to be robust to:

  • Drug names. Real nonproprietary names carry class in their stems by construction: -mab monoclonal antibody, -pril ACE inhibitor, -statin, -olol, -prazole. A model trained on a few thousand drugs with their classes and mechanisms will attach those stems to meaning, and a new -tinib lands near the kinase inhibitors before anyone tells it. Public data: DrugBank open release, ChEMBL, RxNorm with ATC classes, the WHO INN stem list. Molecules are a separate modality; SMILES strings tokenize into fragments the same way, and the same test applies.
  • Retailer and processor names. Train every entity under several noisy renderings: uppercase, truncated at 20 to 25 characters, with store numbers, cities, and processor prefixes (SQ *, TST*, PAYPAL *), with and without spaces. The model then learns which pieces are the identity and which are noise, and a variant it has never seen inherits the meaning of the pieces it shares. Ten thousand retailers is exactly the scale where this starts to pay off.
  • A test that measures it. Generate species (or merchants) whose names embed a morphological marker for their type with some probability, hold them out entirely, and score type inference on the held-out set. At zero probability the answer is chance (our current result); the slope against probability is the model's morphology transfer. Add the drug set as a real-world replicate.

5. Curriculum ladder v2: the experiment, run

Qwen2.5-3B, unsloth LoRA rank 64, lr 1e-4, 800 steps of 16 sequences for every arm, one evaluation for all of them: the section-6 ladder without and with facts in context, a held-out-species induction level, morphology probes, an ICL regression suite of few-shot classification on four public datasets (SST-2, Banking77, DBpedia, Subj; random-symbol and natural labels; chance 43.3), and perplexity. Episodes are symbol-tuning items generated from the creature database: 2 to 5 groups, fresh random labels every time, grouping attribute varied and weakness never used, half with the field guide in the prompt. Replay is the same kind of episode built from AG News, Emotion, TREC and 20 Newsgroups.

What this settles

  1. Teach the task as the injection. One interleaved run gives 100% recall plus in-weights label induction at 59 to 61% (base 36, knowledge-only 40) and transfers that induction to a partition never used in training. Sequential staging reaches the same induction number but loses 3 points of recall, 21 points of yes/no manipulation and 9 points of pairwise reasoning, and its answer format drifts enough that recall with the facts in the prompt drops to 89%.
  2. Symbol-tuning episodes are what protect and improve in-context learning. Declarative text alone lowered public-data few-shot accuracy by 10 points; adding episodes raised it 15 to 21 points above base. Your regression metric should be that suite, and the fix for the erosion seen in section 2 is in the data mix, not in a lower learning rate.
  3. Keep the replay slice. It does not change the universe results; it keeps the induction skill general (78 vs 71 on public data) and holds perplexity at 15 instead of 20.
  4. Latent, low-frequency attributes are still the hard case. Habitat induction never passed 45% in any arm. Attributes shared by few entities need more exposures or an attribute-specific retrieval step.

Each trained arm: 21 to 25 minutes at 840 to 1,170 tokens/s and 8.7 GiB, plus 7 minutes of evaluation over 4,400 items. Not run yet: the same arms on 7B, a drug-name replicate with ATC classes, and the merchant database with statement-style noisy renderings.
