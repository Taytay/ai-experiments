# Graph methods for the entity-label problem

Memo, 2026-09-15, from a literature pass prompted by the owner's observation that the universe is a bipartite graph (entities on one side, attribute values or labels on the other). Written by a research subagent from web sources; paper ids are arXiv unless noted. Question IDs GRAPH-1 to GRAPH-7 are defined in `reports/QUESTIONS.md`; the runnable ones are PLAN rows 28 to 30 and notes on rows 13, 14 and 22.

## Framing

The universe is a multi-relational bipartite graph: 160 entity nodes on one side, attribute-value nodes on the other (8 type, 8 weakness, 6 habitat, diet, region), five relation types, and every relation is *functional* (out-degree exactly 1 per entity per relation). There are no entity-entity edges and no attribute-attribute edges, so every path of length 2 is entity-attribute-entity (E-A-E), i.e. "shares a value". In these terms: L1 recall is 1-hop link prediction (e, r, ?); yes/no and pairwise same-type are E-A-E path existence; the Timmy task is link prediction for a *fresh* relation `label` whose edges are defined by a 2-hop metapath (query -type-> v <-type- demo -label-> "zorp"), with the label nodes relabelled per episode; held-out species with the field guide in context is inductive link prediction with a textual node description (the BLP setting); prototype classification of merchants is node classification on a text-attributed bipartite graph where the only node feature is a string, and unseen merchants are inductive nodes. Morphology (`-orc` predicts Voltrix) is a node feature that happens to be predictive of neighbourhood, which is exactly what NodePiece-style anchor hashing tries to manufacture. The one place the graph view is non-trivial is DATA-1: weakness = f(type) is an attribute-attribute edge the training text never states.

## 1. Knowledge-graph embeddings, transductive and inductive

- Transductive KGE (TransE 2013, ComplEx 2016, RotatE 1902.10197) learns one vector per entity and cannot score an entity not seen in training; Rossi et al. (TKDD 2021, 2002.00819) show across 16 methods that reported performance is concentrated on high-degree entities. Here every entity has degree 5 and the edge being predicted *is* the degree, so a transductive KGE is a lookup table with extra steps.
- Text-based inductive KGC is the relevant family: BLP (Daza et al., WWW 2021, 2010.03496) encodes entities from descriptions with a BERT and scores with TransE; SimKGC (ACL 2022, 2203.02167) does contrastive text-only KGC and beats RotatE on the sparse, description-rich WN18RR (MRR 0.67 vs 0.48) but not on the dense FB15k-237 (0.336 vs 0.338). The lesson transfers: text wins when structure per entity is thin, which is this repo's regime.
- Structure-only inductive models (GraIL, ICML 2020, 1911.06962; NodePiece, ICLR 2022, 2106.12144; ULTRA, ICLR 2024, 2310.04562, zero-shot on 57 graphs on par with per-graph supervised baselines, MRR 0.389 on the 16 transductive graphs; KG-ICL, NeurIPS 2024, 2410.12288, 43 graphs; KGPFN, May 2026, 2605.14907, 57 graphs) all need the unseen entity to arrive *with edges* (a subgraph). A never-trained species with no field-guide entry has none; with the entry it has all of them, and the query becomes a 1-hop lookup. SEMMA (2505.20422, 54 graphs) is the one KGFM that adds LLM text embeddings of relation names and reports a 2x gain when the relation vocabulary is unseen, again saying text carries what structure cannot.
- The morphology result is the NodePiece idea in reverse: NodePiece hashes an entity by k anchors and its relations and stays within Hits@10 of full embeddings with 10x fewer parameters; here the subword tokenizer is the anchor vocabulary, and section 8.4 shows the LM learns the anchor-to-type map only when the data makes it predictive (93.8% vs 4.2%).

## 2. GNNs on bipartite/heterogeneous graphs and label propagation

- LightGCN (SIGIR 2020, 2002.02126) is a linear propagation over ID embeddings on a bipartite graph; the authors note layer 0 *is* matrix factorization, and the +16% over NGCF comes from co-occurrence smoothing over users with many shared items. With functional relations one propagation step from an entity reaches its single attribute value; the second step reaches its co-typed siblings, which is the label itself. There is nothing to smooth.
- RGCN (1703.06103), HGT (2003.01332) and GraphSAGE (1706.02216) need node features or neighbours to aggregate; a species node has one neighbour per relation and a string. A 2-layer GNN over the E-A-E graph would be told the answer through the graph.
- Correct & Smooth (Huang et al., ICLR 2021, 2010.13993) beats the best GNN on ogbn-products with 137x fewer parameters by propagating *labels and residual errors* over the graph. That is the one graph mechanism with a plausible home here, on the embedding side: a kNN graph over transaction-string embeddings, where the same merchant's noisy renderings should sit in one cluster. TPN (ICLR 2019, 1805.10002) and Iscen et al. (CVPR 2019, 1904.04717) show transductive label propagation beats prototypes in the few-shot regime (miniImageNet 5-way 1-shot 53.8 vs ProtoNet 49.4). That is a 4-point effect at k=1 that vanishes by k=5.

## 3. Text-attributed graphs, LMs on graphs, KG injection

Two questions: does the method write the graph into weights or read it from context?

- Reading from context: GLEM (ICLR 2023, 2210.14709), TAPE (ICLR 2024, 2305.19523, 2.88x faster than GLEM on ogbn-arxiv), GraphFormers (NeurIPS 2021, 2105.02605), LLaGA (ICML 2024, 2402.08170), GraphGPT (SIGIR 2024, 2310.13023) all encode a neighbourhood and feed it to the LM/GNN at inference. LLMNodeBed (Wu et al., 2502.00829; 8 LLM methods, 8 classical, 14 datasets, 2,700 models) finds LLM-based node classifiers win only when labels are scarce, and the gap closes with labels. KG-Adapter (ACL Findings 2024), Graph Neural Prompting (AAAI 2024, 2309.15427; +13.5% commonsense on a frozen LLM), KAPING, GraphRAG (2404.16130), ERNIE (1905.07129), K-BERT (1909.07606) and K-Adapter (2002.01808) likewise supply the subgraph at inference or fuse a KG encoder. All of them sit below the repo's "oracle context" line, which already inserts the exact record, and Fatemi et al. ("Talk like a Graph", ICLR 2024, 2310.04560) show graph-to-text encoding alone moves LLM accuracy from 4.8% to 61.8%, so adjacency-list prompting is a worse serialisation of the field guide, not a new capability.
- Writing to weights: the only graph-native recipe is EntiGraph (Yang et al., 2409.07431), which extracts entities, then generates text about *pairs and triples* of entities (random walks on the entity graph) and reports Llama-3-8B closed-book QuALITY going from 39.5 to 56.2 with ~600M synthetic tokens. The K stream's "comparative" texts are a one-hop version of this; the 2-hop walk version has not been run. What the weights then hold is characterised by Hernandez et al. (ICLR 2024, 2308.09124): for 48% of relations the subject-to-object map is a single linear transform on the subject representation (an LRE); the rest are stored non-linearly (per-entity). Whether LoRA injection produces a relation or 136 memorised facts is directly measurable and unmeasured here. On paths from weights: Yang et al. (ACL 2024, 2402.16837) find latent 2-hop in >80% of prompts for some relations and weak second-hop scaling; Balesni et al. (2411.16679) find shortcut-free latent composition ranges from 80% (country) to 5% (year); Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2024, 2405.15071) find composition from weights appears only after grokking and does not generalise OOD. Expect a 3B LoRA to compose E-A-A paths poorly without training on them.

## 4. Graph structure for the embedding side

- StarSpace (AAAI 2018, 1709.03856) embeds entities and labels in one space and trains by ranking; the section 6.3 centroid prototype is StarSpace with the label vector set to the mean of its members, without training. ZestXML-style generalised zero-shot extreme classification adds label *text* so a label with zero examples has a vector; the merchant categories have meaningful names ("Groceries"), the universe labels ("zorp") do not, so this matters only on the merchant side.
- PECOS (JMLR 2022) and XR-Transformer (2110.00685) are label-tree methods for 10^5 to 10^6 labels; with 8 to 30 categories they buy nothing.
- Inductive matrix completion (IGMC, ICLR 2020, 1904.12058) and DropoutNet (NeurIPS 2017) handle cold-start nodes from content features and are the recommender-systems name for "unseen merchant with only a string". Their gain over a content-only model comes from *collaborative* signal (users), which the merchant graph does not have.
- Transductive label propagation over a kNN graph of all transactions (labelled and unlabelled) is the one method that uses structure the merchant data actually has: repeats. Zipf means the head merchant has hundreds of noisy renderings and the tail has one; LP helps the head cluster and cannot help a singleton.

## 5. In-context and few-shot link prediction

- PRODIGY (NeurIPS 2023, 2305.12600) pretrains a GNN over a "prompt graph" connecting demonstrations and queries and reports +18% over contrastive pretraining and +33% over fine-tuning with limited data on citation and KG tasks; KG-ICL and ULTRA do the same without any text. All three solve the Timmy-with-context episode exactly, because with the field guide present it is a 2-hop path query; they say nothing about inducing the rule from *weights*.
- KICGPT (EMNLP Findings 2023) puts retrieved triples into an LLM's demonstrations for KGC and helps on the long tail; that is retrieval, and the repo's oracle-context rows bound it.
- Symbol tuning (Wei et al. 2023, 2305.08298; already reproduced in section 8) and Webb et al. (Nature Human Behaviour 2023, 2212.09196; GPT-3 at human level on matrix analogies) are the relevant "learn the grouping rule" results and are not graph methods; the graph literature adds no mechanism beyond them for relabelled label nodes.

## 6. Where graph methods do not apply

The entity side has no intra-side edges, every relation is functional, and the model already recalls the whole edge list at 100%. Any message-passing scheme on this graph either stops at one hop (a lookup) or reaches co-members at two hops (the label). Node features are the name string, which the LM already reads better than any GNN encoder. Transductive KGE cannot see unseen merchants; inductive KGFMs need the unseen node's edges, which is the oracle context. Graph-to-LM adapters read at inference and are bounded by the oracle rows. Label-tree extreme classification is for 10^6 labels. What survives is: (a) probes of how the graph is stored in the weights, (b) graph-walk augmentation as a *data* recipe, and (c) label propagation on the embedding side where repeated noisy strings supply real structure.

## Not worth it

- LightGCN / RGCN / HGT / GraphSAGE on the universe graph: one hop is the lookup, two hops is the label; no features beyond the name; not inductive.
- GLEM / TAPE / LLaGA / GraphGPT: need neighbours with text and labels on the entity side; there are none. LLMNodeBed shows the LLM gain is a label-scarcity effect the repo already exploits via pretraining.
- KG-Adapter / GNP / GraphRAG / KICGPT: inference-time readers, bounded by the oracle-context rows at 160 records; revisit only at 10k merchants when retrieval quality (REAL-2) becomes the bottleneck.
- ULTRA / KG-ICL / PRODIGY as solvers: the episode graph is a 2-hop path query solved exactly; a symbolic solver is the same 100% line and costs nothing to state in the report.
- NodePiece-style entity hashing: the anchors would be the attribute values; hashing an entity by its relations leaks the answer.
- Transductive KGE (TransE, RotatE, ComplEx) on entity IDs: cannot score an unseen merchant; on seen ones it is the lookup table the adapter already is.
- PECOS / extreme-classification trees: 8 to 30 labels.
- Prompting the LLM with adjacency lists as "graph reasoning": Fatemi et al. show encoding sensitivity dominates; it is a worse rendering of the field guide.
