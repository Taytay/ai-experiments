# Injecting New Knowledge into Large Language Models via Supervised Fine-Tuning

- arXiv 2404.00213 v2, 2 Apr 2024 - https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00213
- Nick Mecklenburg, Yiyou Lin, Xiaoxiao Li, Daniel Holstein, Leonardo Nunes, Sara Malvar, Bruno Silva, Ranveer Chandra, Vijay Aski, Pavan Kumar Reddy Yannam, Tolga Aktas, Todd Hendry (Microsoft)
- Venue: preprint
- Source: references/papers/2404.00213/paper.txt

## One-paragraph summary
The paper studies whether plain SFT on synthetic Q&A pairs can teach GPT-4 (v0613, Sept 2021 cutoff) facts from six Wikipedia articles about sporting events, five of them post-cutoff. It compares two ways of scaling the training set: token-based (generate Q&A per section until 1x/5x/10x the section's token count) and fact-based (extract atomic facts, then 10 Q&A pairs per fact, subsampled to 1x/5x/10x). Token scaling gives large gains at 5x and diminishing or negative returns at 10x, and an embedding-based audit shows that about 20% of atomic facts are never covered even at 10x while a few facts receive more than 25 questions; fact-based scaling gives monotone gains with guaranteed coverage. SFT never beats RAG but gets within 16%, and doubling epochs adds up to 10-15 absolute points, indicating underfitting.

## Problem
Practitioners need to add post-cutoff or proprietary knowledge without RAG. The open question is how to build a training set from documents so that SFT actually injects the facts, and whether naive "more tokens" scaling covers the knowledge uniformly (Sec. 1, 3).

## Method
Articles: 2023 Cricket World Cup, Super Bowl LVII, 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup, 2023 Masters, 2023 NCAA tournament, plus 2018 FIFA World Cup as a pre-cutoff control. Token-based sets are generated with GPT-4 at temperature 1 with a seed Q&A; fact-based sets first ask GPT-4 for atomic facts (with a "SKIP" option for irrelevant facts, App. A), then 10 unique Q&A per fact. Training: LoRA rank 16, batch 1, 3 epochs, loss only on assistant tokens (Sec. 4); hyperparameters were not swept. Evaluation: held-out Q&A generated by the same procedure, GPT-4 binary judge, temperature 0 (Sec. 5). RAG baseline uses Azure hybrid search over the cleaned sections.

## Experiments and results
- Sanity check (Sec. 6.1): base accuracy 0.712 on FIFA 2018 vs at most 0.242 (NCAA) on the post-cutoff articles; Super Bowl is 0.061.
- Token scaling (Fig. 1): 1x more than doubles accuracy on all five post-cutoff articles; 5x adds large, significant gains (PGA +78.6% relative over 1x); 10x gives diminishing returns (NCAA +1% relative) except Cricket (+26.8%); FIFA 2018 degrades at 10x, attributed to overfitting on one Q&A style. SFT is within 16% of RAG everywhere but never above it.
- Coverage (Fig. 2, App. B): with ada-002 embeddings and a 0.945 cosine threshold, ~20% of atomic facts have no covering example even at 10x, and coverage grows much more from 1x to 5x than from 5x to 10x; one fact in FIFA 2023 gets more than 25 questions.
- Fact-based scaling (Fig. 3): no drops at any scale, consistent gains 1x to 5x to 10x, still below RAG; the 10x-vs-RAG gap on post-cutoff articles matches the FIFA-2018-base-vs-RAG gap.
- Cross-evaluation (Fig. 4, Sec. 6.4): token-trained models scored on the fact-based eval lose 12.8% (FIFA 2023) to 37.3% (PGA) relative at 5x and 10x.
- Epochs (Fig. 5, Sec. 7): 6 epochs vs 3 raises PGA 10x by ~10 absolute points and NCAA 1x by ~15; 10x at 3 epochs beats 5x at 6 epochs on all six articles, by 3-5% on some, so data diversity contributes beyond training length.
- Table 1: fact-based sets have roughly 2x the tokens of token-based sets at 1x (1.87-2.66) and 19.7-28.7x at 10x, so the fact-based comparison is partly token scaling in disguise.

## Limitations
One model (GPT-4), one domain, LoRA rank 16 with no tuning and admitted underfitting, no forgetting evaluation, train and eval Q&A from the same generator distribution, no absolute accuracy tables in the text (only relative deltas and figures), and a token-count confound between the two scaling schemes.

## Relevance to this workspace
- DATA-5: this is one of the two sources behind the "saturates near 10 paraphrases" line in LIT-1; what it actually shows is diminishing returns from 5x to 10x token-scaled Q&A, driven by uneven fact coverage rather than a hard limit on paraphrases. Our templated texts cover every attribute of every entity uniformly, which is the fact-based regime; more templates of the same kind should plateau, as Fig. 3 suggests.
- TRAIN-1: coverage imbalance is a gradient-weight imbalance. Logging per-fact (per attribute) label-token counts, not just per-stream counts, is the direct analogue of Fig. 2.
- TRAIN-4: rank 16 underfit and 2x epochs gave +10-15 points; our 400/1600-step sweep is the right control, and the 10x-3ep vs 5x-6ep comparison is the design to separate "more distinct texts" from "more steps".
- EVAL-2 / EVAL-3: their eval is generated Q&A scored by a judge; the cross-evaluation (token-on-fact) is a warning that our ladder items must be built from the full attribute set, not sampled by a generator.
- Cost on one 24 GB GPU: nothing to run from this paper directly; its designs are cheap re-parameterisations of existing arms.
