Late-Interaction Categoriser

The encoder family files a transaction into one of the household's own categories by matching it against the household's history, trained as a decision model rather than a search engine. Thirty rows on, it reads the owner's real budget within 1–2 points of the tuned decider-4B: EmbeddingGemma 2 as two towers reads 71.3 on two seeds (decider-4B v5 72.5 / 73.4) and leads it at top-10 (92.4–92.5 against 90.3–90.6). Its H100 recipe trains in 15 minutes. The 32M Ettin model, fcr, reads 70.8 / 70.9 at 1.9 ms a transaction. The gap left is first-time payees.

Encode once, reuse all dayCategory documents are encoded once per day and shared by every transaction that day. Only the query is new.
Score every option at onceLike Clef and decider, every visible category gets a score in one pass; a softmax over the household's own options is the decision.
Trained as a decisionCross-entropy plus a Brier term. Override episodes teach it that a household's filings beat a payee's name, and hidden history-row labels supervise about 76 more answers per step.
Two towers for a pretrained baseWith EmbeddingGemma 2 the query and each category get one pooled vector each, compared by cosine. That beat MaxSim with this base (71.3 against 70.5), since it was pretrained for one vector.

fcr's recipe (§198 decider remaining training lessons in the late-interaction model): Ettin-encoder-32M after a knowledge and alias stage; 200 v5 training households; override 0.1; hidden labels 0.15; crowd line; 3,000 steps; 1,024 tokens. The Gemma models train on the same data and recipe (§210 EmbeddingGemma 2 as the encoder).

What we learned

Findings since row 215

Payees move

On the owner's budget, when a payee's usual category is wrong, its latest filing is right 43% of the time; in our v4 households it was 18%. v5 households with moves, churn and projects rank ten arms as the owner's budget does (rank correlation 0.83, from 0.04).

§196 A better stand-in for real data
+7.4 from override training

Moving a household's payees to another category, consistently, teaches the model that history beats names. The same query read 59.5 without it and 66.9 with it. It was the largest single lever.

§190 decider training lessons on the late-interaction model
A better stand-in trains a better model

Training on v5 households instead of v4 added 1.5 on the owner's budget, over two seeds that agree within 0.1. decider gained only 0.5 from the same switch.

§197 Training on v5 households · §202
+6 from the crowd line

Other households' filings for the payee lift first-time payees by 6 where a crowd exists. Trained with dropout, it costs nothing where none exists. The owner's budget has no real crowd, so it cannot show the gain.

§198 decider remaining training lessons
Knowledge goes in, not out

A knowledge stage raises held-out kind recall from 3 to 65%, but history training spends it. Distillation from decider keeps real merchant names (+3 to +7 on rational households) without moving the owner's first-time payees. So do kind lines (+6) and cold-start examples (no change).

§191, §192, §194, §208
+2.4 on first-time payees

Using EmbeddingGemma 2 as the base is the first change to reach the owner's first-time payees: 54.5 / 54.4 against fcr's 52.1 / 51.8, and +4–5 at top-3. Synthetic sets do not show it.

§210 EmbeddingGemma 2 as the encoder
Size alone did not pay

Ettin-150M, ModernBERT-base and Ettin-400M know more merchants (+9 to +10 on new rational merchants for 400M) but tie the 32M model on the owner's budget (70.4–70.6). The Ettin-1B option scorer reads it at 39.3.

§199 Larger encoder bases · §205
Names vs repeats

On first purchases decider reads names 10–13 points better than fcr (93.6 against 80.7 for obviously named merchants). fcr matches repeat purchases better (97–98 against 86–95). Category synonyms cost fcr nothing.

§207 Payee and category synonyms
Busy categories are mostly right

Discounting categories by their use helps rational first purchases by at most 2 points. On the owner's first-time payees it costs 2 to 50 points: the pull toward busy categories is learned, and mostly right.

§209 The busy-category correction
Matching aggregates is not enough

v6 households match the owner's realness aggregates. Only routing per payee holds on the owner's budget (+0.5 over two seeds). More new merchants cost 2 points there. Generated data has given what it can.

§206 v6 households
v5 misranks first-time readers

v5 households put fcr above Gemma on first-time payees (67.0 against 66.2); the owner's budget puts it the other way (52.1 against 54.6). A blend weighted on v5 therefore gained nothing (71.2).

§212 EmbeddingGemma 2 on an H100, and the fcr / Gemma blend
What the model sees is enough

Longer, structured queries, options inside the input (GLiClass), per-filing candidates on Ettin, empty categories, evidence-free episodes and 6,000 steps all stay within noise. Several also slow the read.

Next

What is still open

  1. Real data for first-time payees. Generated data has given what it can on these measures (§206), and v5 misranks first-time readers (§212). More real budgets would let first-time choices be made off the owner's budget, which must stay a test set. They would also allow distillation from decider's reads of real budgets (§192).
  2. A real crowd. The crowd line is worth +6 on first-time payees where a crowd exists (§198). Only YNAB's own users' filings can test it on a real budget.
  3. EmbeddingGemma 2 on the 15-minute H100 recipe. Still untested: a lower learning rate or a frozen lower half, to overwrite less of the base. Untrained, it reads obvious names at 74% from the name alone; trained with history, it reads them at 80%, like fcr (§210). Also untested: a second seed of the H100 model.
  4. Route first-time payees. decider for first-time payees and fcr for the rest would read 72.4 against decider alone's 73.0, with decider on 31% of transactions (§208). Whether Gemma for the rest closes that gap has not been read.
  5. Better lookups, not a better model, for kind lines. On the owner's budget the lookups are the limit (§194). Row 160's payee resolution is the lever.
  6. Auto-filing threshold. Fit the chosen model's read temperature and set its auto-file rule by expected cost (§118 Scoring by expected cost). Distillation showed calibration can move (§192).
  7. Parked: fine-tuning Clef (row 164 (b), §204); a second seed of options-in-the-input (§203); Ettin-400M, the strongest model where merchants are known (§199); row 215, a game as a decision task.
  8. Housekeeping: exact Modal costs for rows 216–239 from scripts/modal_costs.py once Modal bills those days. The last report run is from 2026-10-05.

Choices are made on synthetic households and confirmed on the owner's budget once, with two seeds before anything there is called a gain.
