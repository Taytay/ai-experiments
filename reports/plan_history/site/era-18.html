Row 225 · 2026-10-06 · REAL-27 · §196 A better stand-in for real datadone

Finding out why synthetic households ranked models differently from the real budget

Why we ran it

Neither the synthetic v4 households nor the blind_v2 set predicted how the owner's budget ranked the late-interaction models. Both put the dated-history query (p2) near the top; the owner's budget put it last. Override training, worth nothing on synthetic data, was worth +7.4 points on the real budget. We could not choose recipes on data that ranks them wrongly.

What we tried

A script measured properties of the owner's budget, the v4 households and blind_v2 as aggregates only: years of history, category churn, hidden categories, new payee strings, how often a payee's usual category is wrong and whether its latest filing is right instead. A new generator switch, v5, adds what was missing: 8 to 12 years of history, reorganisations that retire a category for a renamed successor, short-lived project categories, and 30% of payees that move for good to another category at a random later date. Then ten already-trained arms were read on ten v5 households and their ranking compared with the owner's.

Example prompt

2019-2023  CITYLINE GYM | $40.00 -> Health
2023-06    (category "Health" retired; "Fitness & Wellness" created)
2024-02    CITYLINE GYM | $45.00 -> Fitness & Wellness
2024-03    CITYLINE GYM | $45.00 -> ?   (right answer: the latest filing, not the usual one)

Illustrative, invented: a v5 payee that moves for good. A model that copies a payee's most common past category is wrong here.

How it did

What we learned

  • The missing property was payees that move. In v4 a wrong "usual category" meant alternation (the payee comes back); in the owner's budget it is mostly a move. That is why override training mattered only on real data.
  • Options matter too: reads must keep a retired category on offer for a year (SPAN_AFTER=365), as decider's reads do.
  • v5 becomes the stand-in for choosing recipes; the owner's budget can be kept for each row's final read.

Row 227 · 2026-10-06 · REAL-27, MODEL-25 · §198 decider remaining training lessons in the late-interaction modeldone

Borrowing decider's remaining training tricks: the crowd line wins, and fcr is born

Why we ran it

The owner asked: "What techniques are missing from these encoders that we used in training our best decider?" Some were already carried over (override training, hidden labels, real-style households, history rows). Others were not: the crowd line (how other households filed the same payee), evidence-free episodes for calibration, coined empty categories, merchant-database merchants swapped into households, alternation episodes and misleading names.

What we tried

Each missing lesson became a training switch. Screen 1 ran on h15's recipe (v4 households) at a 1,024-token limit: evidence-free episodes (ev), empty categories (emp), twice the steps (st6k), the crowd line (cr) and database swaps (dbs). Screen 2 ran the winners on row 226's v5o recipe: fc (control), fcr (crowd line) and fcd (crowd line plus database swaps). One seed each, two for the winner. Alternation and misleading names were not run.

Example prompt

TST* BLUE HERON CAFE | $14.60 | Thu | others: Coffee (12), Dining Out (5)
Nearest: ...
Recent:  ...

Illustrative, invented: the "others" part is the crowd line, counts of other households' filings for the same bank string. It is dropped from 20% of lines and 30% of households in training, so its absence is harmless.

How it did

Arm (% right first)v5 held-outv5 first-timeblind_v2Rational bank, newOwner's budgetOwner, first-time
cr on v4 recipe (screen 1)72.368.081.856.468.8
fc (v5o at 1,024 tokens)74.861.681.558.870.551.1
fcr (+ crowd line), seed 0 / 175.8 / 75.867.5 / 67.381.858.870.8 / 70.952.1 / 51.8

Screen 1's other arms (ev, emp, st6k, dbs) read v5 within 0.6 of the control. The owner's budget has no crowd table, so it cannot show the crowd line's gain. Aggregates only.

What we learned

  • The crowd line is the lesson that transfers: +6 on first-time payees where a crowd exists, and harmless where none does.
  • Evidence-free episodes, empty categories, more steps and a longer limit do not move the stand-in.
  • Database swaps help blind_v2 and new real merchants but cost the owner's budget 0.4 to 1.0. Not kept.
  • Recipe from here is fcr: Ettin-32M, v5 households, override 0.1, hidden labels 0.15, crowd line, 1,024 tokens. Gap to decider-4B: 1.7 points.

Row 228 · 2026-10-06 · REAL-27, MODEL-25 · §203 Options inside the encoder's inputdone

Letting the encoder read the transaction and the options together

Why we ran it

fcr encodes the transaction and each category's document separately and only compares them at the end, so the transaction never "sees" the options. decider reads them in one prompt, as a multiple-choice question. The owner asked: "are you doing any sort of multiple choice thing with the encoders or using cloze style queries? How are you doing 'choose from these options'?" GLiClass, a classifier the owner had pointed to earlier, puts all labels in one input.

What we tried

A joint mode puts the query and every option of the day in one Ettin-32M sequence, each option marked by a separator token whose state is scored against the query; options shuffled in training. jt0 lists option names only; jt3 adds each option's three latest payees. fcr's recipe otherwise (row 227), up to 3,072 tokens, one seed each. A cross-encoder rerank of fcr's top 5 was not run.

Example prompt

BRIGHTWAY PHARMACY | $22.15 | Mon | recent: ...
|| options: [SEP] Bills: Phone [SEP] Health: Pharmacy [SEP] Everyday: Groceries [SEP] Fun: Movies ...

Illustrative, invented: one sequence holds the transaction and every option, so attention can flow between them.

How it did

Model (% right first, one seed)v5 held-outv5 tripsblind_v2Rational, all / new merchantsms a transaction (L40S)
fcr75.847.481.889.0 / 58.8cached documents
jt0, joint, names only76.645.082.488.4 / 62.23.0-7.1
jt3, joint, + 3 payees per option76.334.382.487.6 / 58.73.9-11.7

What we learned

  • Reading options with the transaction helps a little, within one seed's noise: +0.8 on v5, +0.6 on blind_v2, +3.4 on new merchants in the rational households, where a category name must be matched to a merchant.
  • It costs trips (2 to 8 points): in one sequence the option list competes with the dated history rows for attention.
  • Option payees inline add nothing and slow the read. Not read on the owner's budget; kept as a candidate if better training data changes the picture.

Row 229 · 2026-10-06 · REAL-27, MODEL-25 · §201 A structured, longer querydone

Cramming more, better-organised history into the encoder's query

Why we ran it

The owner wanted the query to contain at least "1: examples of payees for each category. 2: Historical record of how we have categorized the payee currently being categorized. 3: Historical record of how we have categorized similar payees ... 4: As much history as possible that we can fit in." The plain query had 8 payees per category document, the 12 nearest earlier filings and 8 recent rows, a median of 439 tokens out of Ettin's 8,000.

What we tried

A sectioned query: "this payee" with counts per category and its last five filings, "similar payees" with 15 rows of other payees, and "recent" with 40 rows (s40) or 100 rows (s100), plus s40m20 with 20 payees per category document. Trained on v5o's recipe with gradient checkpointing for the longer inputs; control fc from row 227.

Example prompt

NORTHGATE HARDWARE | $27.30 | Sat
|| this payee: Home Repair x14 (last 21d ago), Gifts x1 (last 300d ago); ...
|| similar payees: ACE BUILDERS SUPPLY | $55.00 | Home Repair; ...
|| recent: SHELL 4410 | $38.00 | Fuel (1d ago); ... (40 rows)

Illustrative, invented: the sectioned layout, with counts for the payee's own history.

How it did

Arm (% right first)v5 held-out (first-time)v5 tripsblind_v2Rational bank, new
fc (plain query)74.8 (61.6)43.581.558.8
s4074.3 (61.0)50.581.157.7
s10073.8 (60.3)49.581.358.3
s40m2074.3 (60.7)54.979.255.2

Not read on the owner's budget: v5 ranks arms as the owner's budget does, and none beat the control.

What we learned

  • More history does not help: the plain query's nearest filings already include the payee's own when it has any, and its recent rows carry the time signal.
  • Trips gain 6 to 11 points (more recent rows hold more of the trip); everything else loses a little.
  • The owner's gap to decider is 0.2 on known payees and 5 on first-time ones, so history was not the limit. Long queries cost 2x training and about 3x reading time.

Questions it raised

Row 230 · 2026-10-06 · INFRA · §200 Speed for longer queries and larger encodersdone

Why the H100 was slow for encoders, and which GPU each job should use

Why we ran it

An earlier benchmark had put the small encoder on the cheaper L40S because the H100 ran it far slower. With row 229's 1,300 to 5,000-token queries and a 400M-parameter Ettin on the way, the owner asked: "should we be using an L40 for this part? That made sense when it was small!", "Why do we need gradient checkpointing?" and "any unsloth equivalent for these models?"

What we tried

300-step training benchmarks of the long-query 32M recipe and of Ettin-400M on the H100 and the L40S, with PyTorch's default attention (sdpa) and with FlashAttention loaded from the Hub, with and without gradient checkpointing. FlashAttention was checked first: every token vector matched the unbatched read within cosine 0.9997. Read timings on five v5 households (65,353 transactions).

How it did

Ettin-32M, ~1.3k-token queries0.360.412.70.35
Ettin-400M, 1,024-token queries0.89 (checkpointing)1.082.80.41

What we learned

  • The H100's slowness was the attention path: ModernBERT-style encoders with sdpa run about 7x slower on that chip than with FlashAttention.
  • Ettin-32M stays on the L40S (as fast, cheaper); Ettin-400M goes to the H100 with FlashAttention, which also removes the need for gradient checkpointing.
  • Encoders read 3 to 7x faster than decider on the same GPU: roughly $2 (32M), $3.70 (400M) and $11-14 (decider) per million transactions.
  • torch.compile was not measured.

Questions it raised

  • Can a larger embedding model train fast enough to iterate on? (row 238, row 239)

The saved seed-0 model scored decider's own items locally on the RTX 3090, aggregates only: at its training length of 2,048 tokens on all 21,238 items, and at 8,192 tokens (no prompt cut) on every fifth item.

Row 233 · 2026-10-06 · REAL-27 · §206 v6 householdsdone

Closing the last realness gaps in the synthetic households (v6)

Why we ran it

The owner: "I think we can get fcr going much better with better training data". First-time payees were most of the 2.2-point gap to decider (row 231), and row 225 had left three measured gaps: too few first-time payee strings, too many string variants per payee, and too many payees filed under two or more categories.

What we tried

v6 switches, each its own letter: p draws new merchants from the kind's whole pool (about 1,000 places, not the metro's 50 locals); t puts trip purchases at places in the trip's own city; a = p + t with more exploring; b decides purpose routing (a person's, a project's, the catch-all) once per payee, with fewer misfiles and moves; c gives 1-2 string forms per merchant. fcr's recipe trained on each and on combinations, one seed, a second for the best; read on v5, blind_v2, the rational households and the owner's budget. The same row also built shared, content-keyed preparation caches, cutting a screening job to about 15 minutes.

v5:  new merchant drawn from 50 local coffee shops (used up in a few years)
v6p: new merchant drawn from ~1,000 coffee shops nationwide

Row 234 · 2026-10-06 · REAL-27, Q3 · §207 Payee and category synonyms on the rational householdsdone

Testing transfer to unseen merchant names and renamed categories

Why we ran it

The owner: "I want to see if the transfer can happen with payee synonyms, as well as category synonyms". The rational households (row 220: ten obvious categories, every merchant kind always filed to one of them) test whether a merchant's first purchase lands right from its name alone.

What we tried

Same purchases, days, amounts and categories, only the names changed. Payees: real held-out names (as before); invented names that say what they are, never seen in training ("obvious"); and merchants of the same kind that the training households bought from often ("seen"). Categories: as named; obvious synonyms with emoji; looser synonyms that say less. fcr, v6b and v6p (row 233) read locally; then decider-4B on the same households, at the owner's request ("What does decider do with that budget?").

Example prompt

Real held-out name:  POS DEBIT QUILLMARK 0042        (says little)
Invented, obvious:   POS DEBIT HOLLYBROOK PET SUPPLY (says "pets")
Categories as named: Groceries | Pets | Fuel | ...
Obvious synonyms:    Food shop 🛒 | Animal care 🐾 | Gas up ⛽ | ...

Illustrative, invented: the same first purchase under two payee versions and two category versions.

How it did

Payees / categories (% right first: new merchant / repeat)fcrv6pdecider-4B
Real held-out names / as named58.8 / 97.362.8 / 97.770.7 / 86.5
Real held-out names / obvious synonyms59.5 / 97.162.6 / 97.668.0 / 85.7

What we learned

  • Payee transfer works when the name carries the kind: 80% on a first purchase for invented obvious names against 59% for real names that give no hint.
  • Category transfer works: obvious synonyms with emoji cost the encoder nothing.
  • decider transfers names far better on a first purchase (+10 to +13); fcr matches repeat purchases better (97-98 against 86-95), because its alias training ties freshly rendered bank strings together.
  • fcr's misses on obvious names cluster in whole kinds (utilities, pets, cinemas), and it pulls towards the household's busiest categories.

Row 235 · 2026-10-06 · REAL-27, Q3 · §208 Cold-start training examplesdone

Training on examples with no history, to help with brand-new payees

Why we ran it

Row 234 found fcr right on 59-81% of first purchases where decider reached 70-94. The owner: "Let's try to inject knowledge in fcr to have it get better at new payees. Perhaps we show it examples where there is no history?"

What we tried

A share of training queries keeps only the transaction line, with no history rows (COLD); of those, a share also reduces every category document to the category's name alone, as in a brand-new budget (COLDDOC). Arms on fcr's recipe, one seed each: c15 (15% cold), c15d (plus half with name-only documents) and c30d (30% cold). One shared preparation job served all arms, cutting each job from 40 to 19 minutes.

Example prompt

           (no history rows)
