Row 111 · 2026-09-29 · MODEL-16, REAL-22 · §113 Labelled history rows in the promptdone

Prompt format, one change at a time: labelled history rows win

Why we ran it

With the recipe and system settled in row 110, the owner (2026-09-29) proposed prompt-format changes: list categories once with their labels, write each history row's category with its label, check label tokenization, put the weekday next to the date, double the rows.

What we tried

Each change trained and read in its own format, one at a time, against decider-4B's recipe and Ettin-1B's, two seeds each. In decider's usual layout the options come after the question ("Options: (AE) Pets ... Answer: ("). The labelled layouts put the list at the top and end the query with "Category: (". Tokenization was checked: after "- (" and "Category: (" each of the 255 labels is a single token, the one the answer slot reads.

Row 116 · 2026-09-29 · EVAL-10, MODEL-9 · §115 Agreement and calibrated confidencedone

Using encoder and decoder agreement, and is confidence a real probability?

Why we ran it

The owner (2026-09-29) asked whether the two models agreeing could raise accuracy, and whether the encoder's confidence corresponds to a real probability. Row 110 had a cascade (encoder first, then decider); the format rows up to row 113 gave decider its labelled-rows recipe.

What we tried

CPU only, over saved blind_v1 scores: the encoder (three seeds) and decider-4B with labelled rows (two seeds), six pairs. Policies: agreement alone; agree and both confident; agree and either confident; product or mean of the two probability distributions. Calibration checked by reliability (is a 95% answer right 95% of the time?) and ECE (expected calibration error: the average gap between stated confidence and actual accuracy), raw and after a temperature (a single scaling factor) fitted on other users.

How it did

Policy (blind_v1)Auto-filed %Precision %Effort
decider alone (98%)63.397.90.589
Cascade: encoder, then decider66.397.60.570
Both agree (no threshold)83.590.90.640
Both agree and both confident48.998.30.699
Both agree and either confident65.297.80.560
Calibration error (ECE): encoder raw / calibrated; decider raw / calibrated5.6 / 1.9; 7.0 / 2.1

What we learned

  • They agree on 83.5% of transactions and are right 90.9% of the time when they do; when they disagree, decider is right 39.5% and the encoder 21.7%.
  • Agreement alone is not safe to auto-file (91% precision); "agree and either confident" files 65% at 97.8%, the lowest effort so far (0.560).
  • After one temperature fitted on other users, the encoder's confidence is a calibrated probability (its 0.95 to 0.98 answers are right 98% of the time); decider's likewise.

Why we ran it

The split layout from row 117 lets a sync share most of its prompt, but the recipe was trained on rows in plain date order. If the reorder costs accuracy, serving fast would mean retraining.

What we tried

decider-4B's recipe (labelled rows, two seeds) read zero-shot on blind_v1_split and blind_bulk_v1 in both layouts; then the same recipe trained with split episodes (SPLIT=1: the query payee's rows, found by a first-word payee key, moved into a second block), two seeds. The encoder read the bulk set for the system figures.

Row 119 · 2026-09-29 · INFRA-3 · §119 Caching one user's syncdone

Timing a whole sync on one GPU: caching the shared prompt

Why we ran it

The owner asked (2026-09-29): for one user's 10 to 100 new transactions, should we cache the unchanging part of the prompt, or put all the transactions in one prompt with an answer slot each? Row 117 built the sets to measure it.

What we tried

scripts/bench_bulk.py on one H100 with decider-4B and the recipe's adapter, 24 syncs of blind_bulk_v1 (10 and 30 transactions each). Every prompt in a sync begins with the same ~1,330 tokens (the category list and shared rows). Four modes: separate prompts in today's layout; separate prompts in the split layout; the shared prefix computed once and each transaction's tail read from its cache (answers checked against uncached); and one prompt containing every transaction, each with its own answer slot.

What we learned

  • Caching the shared prefix is the answer and needs no training: 2.1 to 2.5 times faster with the same answers (differences at bf16 rounding).
  • One prompt for the whole sync is faster still but, untrained, changes 6 to 7% of answers and costs 1 to 2 points.
  • Rough cost at about $4 per H100-hour: ~$59 per million transactions separately, ~$23 to $28 cached, ~$15 multi-slot (unoptimised stack).

Row 120 · 2026-09-29 · EVAL-12 · §117 A bigger model for hard casesdone

Escalating the hard third to a much larger model

Why we ran it

Row 116's best policy left about 35% of transactions to the user as suggestions. Row 100 had shown the small models' remaining misses on blind_v1 are new users and new businesses, which need world knowledge. A large model might settle what the small ones do not.

What we tried

decider-35B-A3B (Apache-2.0; a 35-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with about 3B parameters active per token), untrained, read blind_v1 zero-shot in its own layout (options after the question, letter labels) on one H100. Then it was used as a third reader for the transactions the small pair did not auto-file. All models calibrated with a temperature and 98% threshold fitted on other users.
