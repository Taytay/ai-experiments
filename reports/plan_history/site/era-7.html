Row 77 · 2026-09-27 · MODEL-14 · REPORT §69done

Trying published "decision" models off the shelf and fine-tuned

Why we ran it

The owner asked (2026-09-26) to try the small decision models that Ollaya serves, and to fine-tune those that allow it. These models pick among options after a long state. The POI-1 layouts of row 72 and the episodes of row 73 gave the test layouts and the training data.

What we tried

Six Apache-2.0 models, each in its own layout, zero-shot:

  • decider-2B and decider-4B (answer read from option-letter logits);
  • Decision-1.0 Sol-2B and Nox-4B;
  • kev-4B (pointer head);
  • Von 1.2 (a ModernBERT encoder).

They ran on REAL-6 with and without the merchant record, on POI-1 (plain and kind lines) and on label induction v2. A sanity set with reversed options checked each readout. Then decider-2B was fine-tuned with its own letter-slot loss on row 73's episodes, and Von's encoder was tried as a start for the row 53 encoder.

Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, the base of nearly every trained categoriser so far, is under the Qwen Research licence. The owner chose (2026-09-26) to move new trained work to Apache-2.0 Qwen3.5. The best recipes to re-check were REAL-6 database episodes and the POI-1 layout of rows 72 and 73.

Qwen3.5-2B and -4B untrained, and trained with the two best recipes: REAL-6 database episodes, and POI-1 descriptions + kind lines + unseen-kind episodes. Fold 0, against the Qwen2.5-3B rows. One answer slot instead of scoring every option

Why we ran it

Row 77 found decider-2B, reading one letter slot over all options, the best POI-1 reader at a tenth of the cost. Was that the layout or the model? The repo's own scorer re-reads the prompt once per option. The comparison used the open base from row 78.

What we tried

Qwen3.5-2B trained in decider's one-slot layout, against the same model with the repo's per-option loss, on the same episodes: REAL-6 database episodes and POI-1's best layout, fold 0. A "shot-label loss" was added, which also trains the label tokens of the 24 examples inside the context. It is the one-slot counterpart of the all-label loss.

Rename augmentation makes the best reader robust to unseen category names

Why we ran it

Row 61 showed readers trained only on real category names lean on what the names mean. Its hypothesis was that adding rename augmentation to the database-episode recipe of row 80 would keep both routes.

What we tried

decider-2B and -4B, one slot + shot-label loss + database episodes, now with RENAME 0.5: in each episode each category name is replaced by a fresh coined word with probability 0.5, consistently. 800 steps, fold 0. Scored on real names and on real6_v1_novel. Two more seeds followed when a later run raised the question of luck.

Row 82 · 2026-09-27 · MODEL-16 · REPORT §81done

Option labels: random letters, and room for 255 options

Why we ran it

The one-slot reader of rows 79 and 80 labels options A, B, C in order. The owner (2026-09-27) wanted labels that do not always start at A and that can reach 255 options. Numbers were dropped because Qwen3.5 has them as single tokens only up to 9.

What we tried

Three label schemes:

  • letters: A, B, C in order;
  • rand26: a random sample of A to Z, in random order, per question;
  • rand255: random picks from decider's 255 single-token labels.

Qwen3.5-2B and decider-2B were trained with each (one slot + shot labels + database episodes, 800 steps, fold 0). The letters-trained models were also read with random labels.

Example prompt

Options:
(K) Plenk
(B) Animals
(W) Quorra
(F) Grendo
[...]
Answer: (

An illustration of rand26: letters drawn at random per question, so no letter always means "first".

How it did

Model, trained / read withREAL-6 top-1 %Novel namesAuto-filed at 98% (achieved)
Qwen3.5-2B, letters / letters85.9–20.1 (95.0)
Qwen3.5-2B, letters / rand25576.5–2.7 (87.5)
Qwen3.5-2B, rand26 / rand2688.983.226.8 (96.2)

  • Letters-trained models never learned "A is first". They bind each letter to its option, though untrained two-letter labels cost 6 to 9 points.
  • Random labels act as augmentation, helping most on novel names and the confident band.

Merchants whose names point to the wrong category

Why we ran it

REAL-6's made-up merchants have meaningless names, so nobody had tested whether a fact in the weights, or the user's own history, beats a name that suggests another category. The owner gave this priority (2026-09-27). The readers came from row 81.

What we tried

mislead_v1 has:

  • 48 made-up misleading names ("Tire Barn" is a restaurant, "Pixel Depot" a grocery store);
  • 48 neutral twins;
  • 16 real brands with misleading names (Kayak, Cricket Wireless).

Half the made-up merchants were added to the fact database. Conditions: the merchant alone, or (out of the database) with two of the user's rows filing it correctly. Readers: decider-4B and -2B (row 81's recipe) and the per-option Qwen3.5-2B, each without and with the set in training. "Pull" is the share of answers that go to the name's category.

Row 84 · 2026-09-27 · REAL-14 · REPORT §83, §85done

Teaching the model to be unsure when there is nothing to go on

Why we ran it

The owner wants the model able to abstain, switchable per request, but only on truly ambiguous merchants, never where the name carries a clue. Row 83 provided evidence-free items: neutral made-up merchants in no database and no history. Row 52 had tested decider's "not listed here" augmentation. The reader was row 81's.

What we tried

Part one, with no training: how confident is decider-4B on evidence-free items? Part two trained decider-4B (row 81 recipe + rand26) with a tenth of training questions about a fresh opaque merchant, in two arms:

  • a "cannot tell from this" option, plus "not listed here";
  • soft uniform targets over the user's categories, with no option.

Two seeds each. False abstentions were checked on real places by name group and on misleading names.

Example prompt

[... 24 labelled rows ...]
Transaction: CKCD 06/13 HALKULOCK LTD | $9.70 | Sat
Category:

An evidence-free item: a made-up name in no database and no history. Nothing in the prompt can decide it.

How it did

decider-4B armEvidence-free: ≥ 0.9 confident"Cannot tell" chosenReal plain names: abstainingREAL-6 top-1 %
No training (part one, seed 0): 15.8% right70%––91.9
No abstention training (two seeds)78.9%0.0%2.0%89.6
"Cannot tell" option44.1%98.7%36.9%88.9

What we learned

  • The best reader did not know when it could not know: on evidence-free items it put 90% or more on its top answer 70% of the time.
  • A trained option learns "unfamiliar merchant", not "no evidence". It abstains on plenty of real names that carry clues.
  • Soft targets lower confidence in proportion to evidence (descriptive names and chains keep theirs), at no REAL-6 cost. This is the recommended route; "not listed here" stays for missing categories.

Questions it raised

  • Soft targets combined with "not listed here" are untested. Soft evidence-free questions went into the combined recipe, row 88.

Row 85 · 2026-09-27 · REAL-15 · REPORT §84done

The user's own filing should beat the database and the name

Why we ran it

Row 83 found that of 2,250 training episodes only 6 put the target merchant's own row among the examples, and no user ever filed a merchant against its database category. The owner asked that training cover a user who disagrees with the name or the database. The base recipe was row 81's.

What we tried

Two kinds of training episode:

  • lookup: the target merchant also appears in the examples, under the target's label;
  • override: the user files a database merchant under a different category from its database one, in the examples and the target.

The test set override_v1 has 684 items in which two of the user's rows file a database merchant either as the database says (agree) or otherwise (override). decider-4B with row 81's recipe + the misleading set + rand26, as a control and with lookup = override = 0.1, two seeds each.

Example prompt

Transaction: CHECKCARD 09/08 AMC THEATRES PORTLAND OR | $14.08 | Sat
Category: Gadgets & Food shopping
[...]
Transaction: TST* AMC THEATRES PORTLAND OR | $42.52 | Thu
Category:

An override item. The database says AMC is entertainment, but this user files it under "Gadgets & Food shopping", and that is the answer.

How it did

ReaderOverride, DB-only chains: top-1 %Override, misleading namesREAL-6 top-1 %
Control (+ random labels)60.875.789.8
+ lookup and override episodes99.598.789.4

What we learned

  • Without this training, the database pulls the answer back in a quarter to a half of override items.
  • With 10% override and 10% lookup episodes, the user's filing wins 98 to 100% of the time, misleading names included, with no REAL-6 cost.
  • "Agree" items stay at 100: the model learned "the user's own rows decide", not distrust of the database.

  • Only two-row histories were tested. One row, or rows that conflict, came up later.
  • Lookup / override 0.1 went into the combined recipe, row 88.
