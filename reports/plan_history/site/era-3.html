Row 24 · 2026-09-15 · TRAIN-3 · REPORT §16done

A proper ruler for "how much did training damage the model"

Why we ran it

Perplexity (how surprised the model is by ordinary text; lower is better) had been measured on a single 249-word paragraph, and row 11 showed it swinging twofold between two runs of the same recipe.

What we tried

A frozen slice of the WikiText-2 test set (29 paragraphs, 4,972 tokens), hashed like the question sets. Every saved adapter was re-scored, with paired differences per paragraph.

Example prompt

The absence of any form of paving between the headstone rows contributes to the simplicity
of the cemetery designs. Lawn paths add to the garden ambiance, and are irrigated during the
dry season ...

The start of one of the 29 WikiText paragraphs the models are scored on.

Row 25 · 2026-09-15 · TRAIN-7 · REPORT §17done

Mix in ordinary text to protect general knowledge

Why we ran it

Row 11 found every arm losing 11 to 16 ARC-Easy points in its first 200 steps, replay of few-shot episodes or not: replay protects only what is replayed. Would replaying general text protect general knowledge? Row 24's WikiText slice gave the measure.

What we tried

Arm Cg: arm C with 5% of sequences turned into 70-word WikiText training-split paragraphs (taken from the episode share), scored every 200 steps. Because those paragraphs are long and fully in the loss, 5% of sequences became 26.8% of the loss.

How it did

MeasurebaseCCg (+5% general text)
WikiText perplexity10.6122.8011.60
ARC-Easy (%)73.559.071.0
Recall, trained format (%)13.1100100
Few-shot suite, made-up labels (%)60.478.181.2
Label induction (%)35.661.247.5
Pair (same type?) (%)51.280.058.8

What we learned

  • A little general text keeps perplexity at the base level and buys back nearly all of the ARC-Easy loss (+12 points, p = 4e-5), with no cost to recall.
  • Induction from the weights fell, but that is one seed each and inside the seed spread later measured in row 10; it may also be the thinner episode share.
  • For a merchant categoriser, where existing knowledge is the product, the replay fraction belongs in the recipe.

Questions it raised

  • Take the 5% from the knowledge stream instead of the episodes: proposed, still queued after row 9.
  • Distillation from the pre-training model as a forgetting repair, compared against this replay: row 31.

Row 26 · 2026-09-15 · TRAIN-6 · REPORT §19done

Make each experiment three times faster

Why we ran it

By mid-afternoon on 2026-09-15 one run took about 50 minutes, with the GPU 26% busy during evaluation. The owner asked for iteration time before more experiments, so this row was taken out of order. It belongs after row 9 (the same mixture-by-tokens change).

What we tried

Evaluation: batch every answer option of every question into large forward passes (up to 64 rows) and compute the output layer only at the option tokens. Training: one 16-sequence batch per step instead of two of eight, and "packing" many short sequences into 2,048-token rows with attention blocked between them (verified directly). Arm C was retrained to check the numbers did not move.

How it did

MeasureBeforeAfter
Full evaluation26.5 min9.1 min (about 5 without extra scorers)
Predictions flipped by the new scorer1.9% (no context), 1.1% (with)
Training speed, arm A (tokens/s)4961,017
Training speed, arm C (tokens/s)8582,133
One arm C run with four check-points63 minabout 20 min

What we learned

  • Both evaluation and training were slow because the units of work were too small.
  • Same-seed reruns of arm C already disagree on 17.9% of predictions; the fast path lands within that spread, so the recipe is unchanged.
  • An offloaded memory-saving option crashed some runs; plain checkpointing became the default.

Questions it raised

  • Short-text arms are still bound by per-step overhead; only bigger batches or fewer steps would help.

Row 27 · 2026-09-15 · BASE-3 · REPORT §18done

Distil the teacher's ranking of the options instead

Why we ran it

Row 7 found the teacher reading the card puts only 6% on the right word, so its strong score is a ranking among options, and copying its free-text distribution taught nothing. Could the ranking itself be copied?

What we tried

Arm P2: every distilled example is a question with an option set. The target is the teacher's score for each option, card in front, renormalised over the options; the student sees the bare question and is trained to match. 680 questions over 136 species (type, weakness, habitat, region, diet), plus episodes distilled the same way and 15% hard-label replay. A first run was discarded after a probe showed the training library ignores left-padding masks in training mode; scoring now pads on the right.

Example prompt

Question: What type is Syaott?
Answer:
  options: Syaott is a Voltrix-type. / ... / Syaott is a Verdane-type. / ... (8 options)
  teacher reads the same with "Field guide: Syaott: Verdane-type, ..." in front

The student learns the teacher's preference over these eight sentences, not its next-word guesses.

How it did

Recall, trained format (%)11.910010091.9
Yes/no (%)42.596.286.258.8
Pair (%)51.291.273.850.0
Label induction (%)36.940.058.831.2

What we learned

  • The ranking distils: 91.9% recall at almost no general-ability cost (0.03 nats; ARC-Easy above the base).
  • It distils only the five question forms it saw: yes/no and pairwise comparison stay near chance, induction in the null band.
  • The teacher's own errors come along (diet: teacher 58.1, student 62.5).

  • Can P2 still read a card in the prompt? No: row 14 found it reads context worse than the base.

Row 28 · 2026-09-19 · GRAPH-1, DATA-1 · REPORT §40done

Look inside: did training write a rule or 136 separate facts?

Why we ran it

A paper (Hernandez et al.) found that for many relations the step from a subject to its attribute is one linear map shared across subjects. If arm C stored "type of" that way, a map fitted on some species should read the type of others. The frozen sets of row 1 and the independent-weakness universe of row 23 allowed the test on both universes.

What we tried

Evaluation only. For each species, take the model's hidden state at the answer position at layers 4 to 36, fit a ridge map to the final layer on 109 species, decode the other 27 through the model's own output head over the eight type names. Two prompt forms (the bare question and the training sentence), with and without the card, for type and weakness, base and arm C, both universes.

Example prompt

trained sentence:  Syaott is a
bare question:     Question: What type is Syaott?
                   Answer:

The probe reads the hidden state at the last token of each prompt.

How it did

Layer4121620283236
Arm C, trained sentence: type of unseen-in-fit species (%)12223752577371
Arm C, bare question13121413171613
Base, trained sentence13121313121212

Chance 12.5. Arm C's own first-token answer on the trained sentence: 76.

What we learned

  • In the trained sentence the adapter wrote type as one shared direction, readable from layer 20 on; the base has no such structure.
  • At the bare-question position there is nothing to read at any layer: the format gap is an absence, not a weak signal.
  • Weakness was weakly stored; on the original universe it could be recovered through the type direction, which the model itself does not do.

Questions it raised

  • The probe said the independent weaknesses were not learned at all; row 30 corrected that: they were written weakly into type-first sentences.
  • Not done: the paper's Jacobian version, a probe at the species token, and the merchant set.

Row 29 · 2026-09-19 · GRAPH-3, DATA-5 · REPORT §41done

Texts that walk from one species to another

Why we ran it

EntiGraph (a 2024 paper) improved closed-book QA by generating text about pairs and triples of entities. Row 16 had found that yes/no and comparison skills come only from one-hop comparative sentences. Would two-hop texts add more? The base recipe is the main mixture (row 2's Needs).

What we tried

Template-written walks over three species linked by shared attributes, with a closing sentence naming the path. Arm Cw: arm C with a ninth of the knowledge sequences replaced by walks (knowledge .40, walks .05, episodes .40, replay .15), 800 steps, one seed.

Example prompt

Faline and Huloz both live in tundra habitats, though Huloz is a Voltrix-type creature.
Huloz and Cynome are both found in Brakka, though Cynome is a Tidewell-type creature.
Question: How are Faline and Cynome related?
Answer: Through Huloz. Faline and Huloz share a habitat, Huloz and Cynome share a region.

One generated two-hop walk text.

How it did

% right (ppl: lower is better)C, same code pathCw with walksC, 1,600 steps
Yes/no87.588.897.5
Pair73.877.591.2
Label induction56.959.481.2
WikiText perplexity22.123.420.9

What we learned

  • Walk texts change nothing that same-seed noise does not cover; they are read like more comparative sentences.
  • The headroom is in training steps, not in the texts' graph structure.

Questions it raised

  • Not run: walks at a larger share, LLM-written walks, walks as the whole knowledge stream.

Row 30 · 2026-09-19 · GRAPH-4, DATA-1 · REPORT §42done

Can the model state a rule it only ever read inside sentences?

Why we ran it

In the original universe every species of a type shares one weakness. Did the weights turn that into a rule ("type T is weak to W"), or keep 136 separate facts? New items were added to the frozen sets of row 1, for both the original and the independent-weakness universe of row 23, to follow up row 28's probe.

What we tried

Evaluation only. A bare rule question per type (eight items) and a "path" question per species that states its type and asks its weakness (136 items). Scored on saved adapters. A correction on the way: two training templates do state the rule inside a sentence ("like all T-types it is weak to W").

Example prompt

bare: Question: What type are Voltrix-type creatures weak to?
      Answer:
path: Javish is a Frostel-type creature. Question: What type is Javish weak to?
      Answer:
  options: Voltrix / Pyrrhan / Tidewell / Lithor / Verdane / Umbrine / Gustral / Frostel

The two item forms; the path form gives the type and asks the weakness.

How it did

ModelBare rule: types right of 8Path: % right (chance 12.5)
base00
A knowledge only014.7
C064
C, rerun469.1
D25.1
C, 1,600 steps168.4
C, independent weakness039.7

What we learned

  • A rule read 136 times as a clause is not answerable as a rule (0 to 4 of 8).
  • Given the type in the trained sentence's frame, the weakness comes out (64 to 69). The base and arm D just copy the stated type.
  • On the independent universe the answers follow each species' own weakness (39.7), not a type rule: the weights hold facts per species, reached through the frame they were written in.
  • For a categoriser: a rule written as a clause ("like all grocery stores, X sells food") does not become a generalisation the model can state.

Questions it raised

  • Would a rule stated as its own sentence become answerable? Not run.

Row 31 · 2026-09-18 · TRAIN-8 · REPORT §31done

Repairing forgetting by distilling from the model as it was before injection

Why we ran it

"Arm C" is the project's main knowledge-injection recipe: a rank-64 LoRA adapter (a small trainable add-on to the frozen weights) on Qwen2.5-3B, trained on sentences about 136 invented species plus labelling exercises. It learned the facts but damaged the model's general ability (WikiText perplexity 10.6 to 22.8, ARC-Easy 73.5 to 58.5); row 8 was the last row to measure other arms against it. Row 25 mixed general text back in ("arm Cg") and recovered part of it. A Thinking Machines blog post reported that on-policy distillation (the student imitates the original model on its own samples) restored chat ability while keeping injected knowledge, so we tried it.

What we tried

After arm C, sample the adapted model on general prompts (FineWeb-Edu prefixes or Tulu-3 questions; 64 prompts x 4 samples of up to 96 tokens per step) and push it, token by token, toward the same model with the adapter switched off, using the exact reverse KL divergence as the loss, for 120 steps. A third run also fed one batch of arm C's own training mixture each step ("replay"), so the facts kept being rewritten during the repair. The full evaluation ladder was scored before and after.

Example prompt

Question: What type is Syaott?
Answer:
  options: " Syaott is a Voltrix-type." / " Syaott is a Pyrrhan-type." / ... / " Syaott is a Verdane-type." (8 options)

A fact-recall item from the species ladder in the trained sentence format; the model's choice is the option it scores highest.

How it did

fact recall, trained format (%)13.110010037.596.9
yes/no about the facts (%)42.577.573.846.277.5

What we learned

  • Plain distillation returns every general measure to the base within about 30 steps, and erases the facts at the same time (recall 100 to 37.5 and 33.8). Repair and erasure are one process.
  • The adapter is not zeroed; it keeps its size and rotates (cosine 0.82 with arm C's), cancelling its effect on ordinary text, and the fact directions go with it.
  • With arm C's mixture kept in the loop, the repair keeps the facts and restores general ability: the best injected adapter in the report, ahead of general-text replay on every measure, at about eleven times its compute.

Questions it raised

  • Sampling with Hugging Face generate made this slow (218 of 318 minutes); a faster sampler or a rented H100 was the first thing to change (row 34 moved GPU work to Modal).
  • Not run: the repair on the 1,000 and 5,000-species adapters, a lower rate or early stop, and a replay-weight sweep.
  • Row 38 later found the teacher here had silently been the 4-bit base, a mismatch noted as a correction.

Row 32 · 2026-09-18 · TRAIN-9 · REPORT §32done

Does a LoRA adapter want ten times the learning rate?

Why we ran it

Thinking Machines' "Tinker" LoRA primer says adapters want about ten times the full fine-tuning learning rate. Every decoder adapter here trained at 1e-4, the sweep of row 17 stopped at 2e-4, and in row 18 the Flan-T5 adapter at 3e-4 learned nothing while full fine-tuning at the same rate recalled 98%. If the primer held, those verdicts were artefacts of a too-low rate.

What we tried

Four runs: arm C (Qwen2.5-3B, rank 64, alpha 128, 800 steps) at 5e-4 and 1e-3, and the Flan-T5-large knowledge-only adapter at 1e-3 and 3e-3, all scored on the same ladder of fact, reasoning and general-ability items.

Example prompt

Timmy labels his creature cards with his own made-up tags. He labeled his Nolvan 'FooFoo' and his Noline 'tandle' and his Yoreel 'snerk'. Following the same rule, how is he likely to label his Dolorc?
Answer:
  options: " FooFoo" / " tandle" / " snerk"

An induction item: the model can only find Timmy's rule by using the species types it was taught (Dolorc shares Nolvan's type).

How it did

What we learned

  • For the decoder the usable window is 1e-4 to 2e-4. At 5e-4 the adapter stores the facts but can no longer use them; at 1e-3 the model is destroyed within 200 steps.
  • The primer is stated for a fixed alpha of 32; our alpha = 2 x rank scales updates four times larger, so our 1e-4 already sits near the primer's rate. Step count, not rate, stays the lever.
  • The Flan-T5 adapter at 1e-3 learns a third of the facts (34.4) and loses natural-label in-context learning (87 to 52.6); at 3e-3 it learns nothing. Full fine-tuning at 3e-4 remains that model's recipe.

Questions it raised

  • Not run: alpha 32 with a rescaled rate (the direct test of the scaling explanation), rates between 2e-4 and 5e-4, and T5Gemma at 1e-3.
