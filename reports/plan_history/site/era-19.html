Row 236 · 2026-10-07 · MODEL-17, REAL-27 · §210 EmbeddingGemma 2 as the encoderdone

Fine-tuning a new off-the-shelf embedding model, EmbeddingGemma 2

Why we ran it

Google released EmbeddingGemma 2 (Apache-2.0) the day before: a 271M text model built from Gemma 4, with an LLM's knowledge of the world behind its vectors. Row 235 had concluded that knowing what a name means is what fcr lacks. The owner: "Should we just be running an embedding model at this point like the new Gemma? ... we could fine tune it to know which payees and which categories map together"; "Either cosine similarity or two towers approach".

What we tried

(a) Untrained: the cosine between a first purchase's bank string and each category name in the rational households, no history, against other encoders. (b) fcr's recipe with EmbeddingGemma 2 as the base, two ways: two towers (one vector for the query, one for each category, compared by cosine) and multi-vector (MaxSim, as fcr). One seed, a second for the winner, on L40S GPUs; read on synthetic sets, the rational households and the owner's budget.

Example prompt

Query tower:    "task: classify | ROLLING PINES GOLF CLUB | $65.00 | Sun | recent: ..."
Category tower: "Recreation: ROLLING PINES GOLF CLUB, LANEWAY BOWL, ..."
Score:          cosine(query vector, category vector)

Illustrative, invented: the two-tower layout. Category vectors can be cached; only the query is encoded per transaction.

What we learned

  • A base with an LLM's knowledge helps the owner's first-time payees: +2.4 at top-1 and +4-5 at top-3, two seeds agreeing to 0.1; the first-time gap to decider falls from 5 to 2.7 points.
  • The right answer is in its top 5 and top 10 more often than decider's.
  • Synthetic data does not show the gain: it is knowledge of real merchants and category names, which our generated sets do not reward.
  • Two towers beat multi-vector with this base, which was pretrained for one pooled vector. It is 6-8x slower to read than fcr.

Questions it raised

  • Can unsloth or LoRA train it faster or keep more of the base? (row 238)
  • Would fcr and EmbeddingGemma blended per transaction beat either? (row 239)

Row 237 · 2026-10-07 · REAL-27 · §209 The busy-category correctiondone

Discounting the household's busiest categories for new payees

Why we ran it

Row 234's checks found that on the rational households' first purchases fcr picks the household's most-used category 45.8% of the time, where that is right only 33.5% of the time, and 40% of its misses go there. The owner: "I do want to try to fix the frequency thing".

What we tried

No retraining. On a first-time payee string, each category's score is reduced by tau times log(1 + its filings so far), so busy categories lose some of their pull. fcr swept over tau 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 on the rational households and the owner's budget.

Example prompt

New payee: VALLEYVIEW CINEMA | $24.00
Groceries  (812 filings): score 3.10 - tau x log(813)
Fun        (41 filings):  score 2.95 - tau x log(42)

Illustrative, invented numbers: with tau 0.25 the less-used "Fun" overtakes "Groceries".

What we learned

  • On a real budget, the busy categories are where new payees usually go: the correction costs the owner's first-time payees 2 to 50 points.
  • The rational households, where ten categories share purchases by fixed kinds, overstated the problem; the gain there is at most 2 points.
  • What is left for first purchases is knowledge of what a name is.

Row 238 · 2026-10-07 · MODEL-17 · §211 EmbeddingGemma 2 under unslothdone

Training EmbeddingGemma 2 with unsloth and LoRA

Why we ran it

EmbeddingGemma 2 took 64 minutes to train on an L40S (row 236). The owner: "That article I sent recommended unsloth to fine tune it"; "I want faster training"; "No need for full fine tuning". unsloth's releases capped the transformers library below the version EmbeddingGemma 2 needs, so a small runner script had to override the cap.

What we tried

Full fine-tuning through unsloth's loader trained to NaN at step 1 in every precision tried. unsloth's documented recipe, LoRA (small trainable add-on matrices, rank 32, on all projections) with its own checkpointing, trained cleanly; adapters were merged at save. Row 236's two-tower recipe at learning rates 1e-4 and 2e-4, one seed each, L40S.

What we learned

  • LoRA keeps more of the base's behaviour on generated data (+1-2 on v5, +4 on rational first purchases, trips recovered) but less of the owner's gain: first-time payees 1.4 and top-10 1 point below the full fine-tune.
  • unsloth is not a speed-up here (54 against 64 minutes for 3,000 steps): the cost is the forward and backward pass over about 12,000 query tokens a step. It does fit on the RTX 3090.
  • The full fine-tune stays the recipe for the owner's budget; LoRA is the option when memory is the limit.

Questions it raised

  • Is an H100 without checkpointing the faster route? (row 239)

Row 239 · 2026-10-07 · MODEL-17, REAL-27 · §212 EmbeddingGemma 2 on an H100, and the fcr / Gemma blenddone

EmbeddingGemma 2 in 15 minutes, and blending it with fcr

Why we ran it

Two follow-ups to row 236, approved by the owner ("Go for it - 1 and 2"). First, speed: row 238's unsloth route had not been faster. Second, the two readers have different strengths: fcr is slightly better on payees the household has filed before, EmbeddingGemma 2 on first-time payees. ContextGNN, a recommender paper, uses a local pair-wise score where the user's own history reaches and a tower score beyond it.

What we tried

(a) Row 236's two-tower full fine-tune retrained on an H100 without gradient checkpointing, for 1,500 steps instead of 3,000. (b) No training: fcr and EmbeddingGemma 2 blended per transaction, log p = w log p_fcr + (1 - w) log p_Gemma, with one weight for payees seen before and another for first-time payees, both chosen on 20 v5 held-out households and then read once on the owner's budget.

Example prompt

Transaction: MERIDIAN TACKLE SHOP | $19.99 | first-time payee
fcr:   Hobbies 0.42 | Gifts 0.31 | ...
Gemma: Hobbies 0.55 | Sporting 0.20 | ...
Blend: w = 0.8 for first-time payees, 0.5 for payees seen before

Illustrative, invented: the blend combines both readers' probabilities with a weight that depends on whether the payee is new to the household.

On v5, fcr was the better first-time reader (67.0 against 66.2); on the owner's budget the reverse (52.1 against 54.6). With the owner's own best weights (not a fair read) the blend reaches about 71.6. Aggregates only.

What we learned

  • The H100 recipe holds: half the steps without checkpointing reads the owner's budget as well (71.1, first-time 54.9), and EmbeddingGemma 2 now trains in 15 minutes.
  • The blend gains nothing: v5 misranks the two readers on first-time payees, so the weight it picks costs 1.5 points there. Not pursued.
  • v5 rewards history-matching more than knowing what a merchant name means, so it cannot tune anything first-time-specific; the owner's real data and the rational households are the sets for that.
  • Read times: fcr 1.9 ms a transaction on an H100, Gemma 3.9, decider 10-13 at best.

Questions it raised

  • With the encoders about 1-2 points under decider-4B on the owner's budget, does the remaining first-time gap need real data rather than another synthetic generator?
