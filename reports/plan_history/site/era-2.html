Row 13 · 2026-09-16 · MODEL-3, MODEL-5, BASE-4, BASE-5, GRAPH-5 · §24 Embedding block resultsdone

Embedding models on the LLM's own questions

Why we ran it

Earlier embedding results used only the tiny MiniLM encoder, a different test protocol and no simple baselines. Row 5 also left open whether MiniLM's transfer from clean names to upper-case bank strings survives a case-sensitive encoder.

What we tried

Three encoders (MiniLM 22M, bge-base 109M, Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B; later EmbeddingGemma and gte-modernbert) were trained for seconds to place a species name near its attribute text. On the same induction items the LLM answers, the encoder picks the demo card whose name is nearest the query. Baselines: logistic regression and SetFit. On merchants: new vocabulary tokens with four initialisations, and a knowledge-graph scorer (DistMult) on top of the encoder.

Example prompt

LLM sees: He labeled his Nolvan 'FooFoo' and his Noline 'tandle' and his Yoreel 'snerk'.
          Following the same rule, how is he likely to label his Dolorc?
Encoder sees: embed("Dolorc") vs embed("Nolvan"), embed("Noline"), embed("Yoreel")
              -> nearest card's label

One induction item answered two ways: the LLM reads a prompt; the encoder compares name vectors.

How it did

% rightMiniLMbge-baseQwen3-EmbLLM arm CLLM C + card
Label induction (3 labels)79.494.492.561.970.6
Habitat grouping53.172.580.029.465.6
Merchant category from bank string (12-way)69.884.426.0n/a

What we learned

  • For "which card is this most like", a fine-tuned encoder beats the 3B LLM's weights and its reference card, at 1/140th of the size and seconds of training.
  • Logistic regression ties the plain centroid; SetFit is worse on an already-trained encoder.
  • New vocabulary tokens lose on every encoder, pooling and initialisation; a warm-up of the new rows closes most of the gap.
  • The bank-string transfer is not about the tokenizer's casing (EmbeddingGemma, case-sensitive, reads 77.1); it depends on whether the encoder treats case as a feature.
  • The graph scorer adds nothing over the encoder's own similarity.

Questions it raised

  • Does this hold on realistic, imbalanced transaction histories? Row 22.

Row 14 · 2026-09-16 · BASE-1, REAL-2, GRAPH-7 · §25 Retrieval versus oracle contextdone

Real retrieval instead of pasting the right card

Why we ran it

Every "with context" number so far pasted the exact correct card into the prompt. A model trained that way may do worse when retrieval returns wrong documents. Retrieval from a bank string to a merchant record had never been measured. The Needs entry is row 7; the retriever is the fine-tuned MiniLM of row 13.

What we tried

A MiniLM retriever over the field guide. Four contexts: none, the correct card, the retriever's top 3 per name, and a list of same-type species with no attribute words. Models: base, arm C, the distilled arm P2, and arm Cr (arm C trained with retrieval-style context that mixes the right card with two distractors, "RAFT"). For merchants: recall@1 of a bank string to its own record among 120, then an untrained model reading the retrieved record.

Example prompt

Related creatures:
Dolorc: Javott, Mordor, Draoz
Nolvan: Kelash, Mordor, Velsup
Noline: Ithvan, Skaldor, Gribilk
Yoreel: Cynome, Morurn, Falott

Timmy labels his creature cards ... He labeled his Nolvan 'FooFoo' and his Noline 'tandle'
and his Yoreel 'snerk'. Following the same rule, how is he likely to label his Dolorc?

The neighbour-list context: same-type species per name, no type words at all.

How it did

Label induction (%)nonecorrect cardretrieved top-3neighbour list
base35.649.441.936.9
C61.970.678.187.5
Cr (RAFT-trained)68.869.485.693.1

Merchants: record-tuned retriever recall@1 = 100. Untrained 3B from bank strings with the top-1 record: 81.2 / 75.0 (trained / held-out merchants); with top-3 records: 46.9 / 41.7.

What we learned

  • Retrieved context beats the "oracle" card on induction, because retrieval returns three paraphrases of the same facts.
  • RAFT training adds 3 to 7 points at no cost (one seed).
  • A list of related entities is the best context for trained models and worthless for the base: the trained model uses the entity graph.
  • Give the model one retrieved record, not three: two wrong records halve accuracy.
  • The distilled arm P2 reads context worse than the base; do not pair it with retrieval.

Questions it raised

  • The 10k-record version with realistic frequencies and noisy strings: row 22.
  • A second seed for Cr would make its gain a claim.

Led to

Row 15 · 2026-09-16 · MODEL-1 · §26 Scale effects at 1.5B-7Bdone

Does the recipe hold at 1.5B and 7B?

Why we ran it

Every result was on Qwen2.5-3B, and the literature treats label induction as a scale effect. Row 10 had fixed which 3B gaps were real, giving a band to read other sizes against.

What we tried

Base, A, C and D on Qwen2.5-1.5B (LoRA) and Qwen2.5-7B (QLoRA: the base weights stored in 4 bits so it fits on the 24 GB card), same data, steps and settings, one seed each.

How it did

Gap (points)1.5B3B7B
C − A, label induction+26.2+21.9+18.8
C − A, yes/no−30.0−22.5−3.7
C − A, few-shot suite (made-up labels)+25.0+31.2+30.8
C − base, ARC-Easy−16.0−15.0−11.0
D recall, trained format (%)91.993.165.6

What we learned

  • The mixture's induction and few-shot gains hold at every size; the natural-label few-shot score is flat everywhere.
  • The manipulation trade (knowledge-only answers yes/no better) shrinks to 4 points at 7B, though that is one seed against a 12.7-point seed spread.
  • Every trained arm loses 10 to 15 ARC-Easy points at every size: scale does not remove the forgetting cost.
  • Staged training at 7B loses a third of the facts; the 10% replay fix of row 12 is needed there.
  • 7B evaluation needed a smaller scoring batch because the Windows driver spilled memory.

Questions it raised

  • All 1.5B and 7B numbers are single seeds; the 3B seed spread is the band to read them against.

Row 16 · 2026-09-16 · DATA-5 · §27 Augmentation scaling limitsdone

How many rewordings does a fact need, and which kinds?

Why we ran it

Earlier work credited a doubling in recall to "augmentation" (rewording each fact many times) on the strength of one comparison, and the literature disagreed on whether ten paraphrases or three are needed. Row 8 had just shown one masked text gives backward recall that forward paraphrases do not.

What we tried

Arm A (knowledge text only, 800 steps of 16 sequences, Qwen2.5-3B) with the knowledge stream varied: the first 1, 3, 7 or 14 templates per species; then the 14 plus sentence-order shuffles, plus four backward-written sentences, plus 16 to 28 sentences written by an LLM, or plus the negative and comparative sentences. A fixed budget means fewer templates get more passes each.

Example prompt

forward:   Syaott is a Verdane-type creature. It lives in cave habitats, eats as an piscivore, and is found in Ostrand.
backward:  A Verdane-type with an piscivore diet, native to Ostrand: that is Syaott.
negative:  Question: Is Syaott a Lithor-type?
           Answer: No, Syaott is a Verdane-type, not Lithor-type.
compare:   Syaott and Frodux are both Verdane-type creatures.

Constructed for illustration from the report's templates; not a logged input. Training texts of four kinds, filled from the real templates (the second species and wrong type are examples).

How it did

% right (ppl: lower is better)1 tmpl371414 + backward14 + neg/compare
Recall, trained format73.810097.5100100100
Yes/no46.257.546.2555598.8
Pair (same type?)5058.850505093.8
Backward, hard23.125.631.924.469.419.4
WikiText perplexity (base 10.6)20.523.128.151.347.737.7

What we learned

  • Recall saturates at three distinct wordings.
  • Manipulation (yes/no, comparing two species) comes only from negative and comparative sentences, never from more paraphrases.
  • Backward recall comes only from backward-written sentences: four plain sentences beat row 8's masked training (69.4 vs 48.1).
  • More variety at a fixed budget costs more general ability; LLM-written prose is the cheapest volume.

Questions it raised

  • Do two-hop texts linking three species add more than one-hop comparisons? Row 29.

Row 17 · 2026-09-16 · TRAIN-4 · §28 LoRA hyperparameter sweepdone

Adapter size, learning rate and training length

Why we ran it

Rank 64, learning rate 1e-4 and 800 steps had been fixed in every arm, and one full fine-tune of the 3B had run out of memory. Is LoRA the right vehicle at all? The seed spread from row 10 gave the band to judge each change.

What we tried

Arm C with one change at a time: rank 16 or 256 (adapter size), learning rate 5e-5 or 2e-4, 400 or 1,600 steps, LoRA on the feed-forward (MLP) layers only, and full fine-tuning of all 3.09B weights with 8-bit AdamW at 1e-4 and 1e-5. One seed each.

How it did

Arm C variantRecall, trained fmt (%)Yes/no (%)Label induction (%)WikiText ppl
Baseline (r64, 1e-4, 800 steps)10077.561.922.8
Rank 161005543.119.0
Learning rate 5e-510073.844.417.9
Learning rate 2e-410096.263.828.6

Full fine-tune, 1e-41005040.694.4
Full fine-tune, 1e-519.44532.511.7

What we learned

  • Rank, rate and steps act as one dial: turned down, the adapter stores facts it cannot use; turned up, it uses them and forgets more.
  • 1,600 steps improved both sides (better use, lower perplexity); later read against the seed spread, only the perplexity gain clears it.
  • Rank 256 buys nothing; MLP-only LoRA matches all layers at lower cost.
  • Full fine-tuning found no working rate: 1e-4 wrecks the model, 1e-5 learns nothing in 800 steps.

Questions it raised

  • LoRA at ten times the rate: row 32.
  • Longer training for the merchant categoriser: row 47.

Row 18 · 2026-09-16 · MODEL-2, REAL-3 · §29 Encoder capacity and entity countdone

Encoder models, and 1,000 to 5,000 species in one adapter

Why we ran it

Two questions met here. Would a fill-in-the-blank encoder be a better vehicle for injecting and extracting facts? And does 100% recall survive when one adapter must hold far more entities than 136? Both build on the main mixture experiment (row 2's Needs).

What we tried

Arm C on universes of 1,000 and 5,000 species at 800 and 4,000 steps. ModernBERT-large (an encoder) answering by predicting a letter at a mask. Flan-T5-large and T5Gemma (encoder-decoders) trained to fill masked spans of the knowledge texts, at several learning rates, with full fine-tuning or a rank-64 adapter.

Example prompt

input:  Syaott is a <extra_id_0>-type creature. It lives in cave habitats, eats as an <extra_id_1>, and is found in Ostrand.
target: <extra_id_0> Verdane <extra_id_1> piscivore

The span-prediction format for the T5 models: attribute values are masked behind sentinel tokens (positions illustrative).

How it did

SettingRecall, trained format (%)
Arm C, 1,000 species, 800 steps (0.3 passes)51.2
Arm C, 1,000 species, 4,000 steps (1.5 passes)100
Arm C, 5,000 species, 4,000 steps21.9
ModernBERT-large, letter at mask9.4
Flan-T5-large, 1e-4 (the decoders' rate)16.9
Flan-T5-large, full fine-tune 3e-498.1 (100 written)
T5Gemma, rank-64 adapter, 1e-497.5 (93.1 written)

What we learned

  • At 1,000 species the limit is exposure, not capacity: given 1.5 passes the adapter holds them all (manipulation 97.5 / 86.2), at an hour of training and higher perplexity (41.3).
  • At 5,000 species the budget runs out first; equal-exposure comparison (51 vs 22) is a first hint of interference.
  • The encoder-only letter protocol learns nothing at this size.
  • The encoder-decoders learn the facts only at their own learning rate, show no label induction, and lose general ability (natural-label few-shot: Flan-T5 75.0, T5Gemma 43.8) in a way the decoder mixture does not.

Questions it raised

  • 5,000 species at 1.5 passes (20,000 steps) was sent to cloud GPUs in row 34, then overtaken; row 59 asks capacity on the merchant database.
  • The Flan-T5 adapter at a higher rate: row 32.

Led to

  • Row 32 · Does a LoRA adapter want ten times the learning rate?
  • Row 34 · Moving GPU work to cloud H100s on Modal

Row 19 · 2026-09-17 · BASE-2 · §30 Knowledge editing for factsdone

Knowledge editors: write facts straight into the weights

Why we ran it

Dedicated editing methods (MEMIT, AlphaEdit) promise to insert facts with no damage elsewhere, the property fine-tuning lacked. How do they compare on the same ladder as the fine-tuned arms of the main experiment (row 2's Needs)?

What we tried

EasyEdit's MEMIT and AlphaEdit on Qwen2.5-3B, all edits in one batch, layers 4 to 8. First one edit per species (its type, 136 edits), then five per species (680 edits), then variants that name the relation before the species, and record-style prompts. The edited models were scored like any arm.

Example prompt

edit request:  "Syaott is a"  ->  "Verdane-type creature"
five-fact variant, relation first:  "As a weakness, Syaott is weak to" -> "Frostel-type attacks"

Constructed for illustration from the report's templates; not a logged input. Edit requests are the ladder's own sentences with the species at the front.

How it did

Measurebasearm CMEMIT as shippedMEMIT + ridge 15AlphaEditAlphaEdit, 5 facts
Recall, trained format (%)13.11008.110010048.8
Plain "What type is X?" (%)17.919.612.5100.0100.016.1
Yes/no (%)42.577.547.55557.542.5
ARC-Easy (%)73.558.5237271.574.5
WikiText perplexity10.622.85.7 × 10710.710.910.7

What we learned

  • An editor writes one association per species perfectly, answers the plain question too (fine-tunes read about 20), and costs nothing in general ability.
  • It writes nothing else: no yes/no, no comparison, no induction, no backward recall. It even answers the weakness question with the type (0.0).
  • MEMIT as shipped destroyed Qwen2.5-3B: half its covariance eigenvalues are near zero. A small ridge term fixes it.
  • Several facts per species collide on one key and blur; naming the relation first recovers part of each (46 / 36 / 69 on type / weakness / habitat).

Questions it raised

  • One target vector per species optimised over its five facts is untried.
  • For a merchant fact database, an editor stores one association, not a record; the record route goes to the categoriser in row 33.

Row 20 · 2026-09-18 · EVAL-4, EVAL-5 · §33 Item rebuild diagnosticsdone

Rebuild the tests so they can only be passed one way

Why we ran it

A reviewer objected that the induction questions might not pin down which rule they test, and that the few-shot regression suite shared its label generator and template with training, so its good scores might be familiarity. Both concern the instrument built in row 1.

What we tried

No training. New induction items with two demos per group that share only the intended attribute (type, habitat, diet or region), plus an "unseen label" variant whose right answer is the one label no demo carries. A new few-shot suite with a Q/A frame and rare English nouns as labels. A 200-question, 5-shot MMLU slice as a second forgetting check. All saved models re-read on them; the old items were also split by whether a competing rule agreed.

Example prompt

Timmy labels his creature cards with his own made-up tags. He labeled his Javish 'tandle'
and his Briath 'dabble' and his Tunurn 'dabble' and his Zarash 'tandle'. Following the same
rule, how is he likely to label his Drarock?
Answer:   (tandle / FooFoo / dabble)

The unseen-label item: Drarock's type matches neither demo group, so the right answer is the label nobody used (FooFoo).

What we learned

  • Grouping by type from the weights is real on items that pin the rule down; grouping by any other attribute is at chance, except a weak signal in arm D.

  • No model ever chooses a label that no example carries: the learned skill copies the label of the most similar example.

  • For the categoriser: unseen category names need the name in the prompt or retrieval, not more fine-tuning.

Row 21 · 2026-09-18 · DATA-3 · §34 Morphology realism transferdone

Does a name fragment become a usable clue?

Why we ran it

The main experiment's morphology arm E (built on row 2's Needs) classified never-seen names ending in a type's suffix at 93.8%. A reviewer had three doubts: the test names reused trained name parts; only one marker rate (0.7) was tried; and real markers are often at the start of a word (drug stems, "AMZN Mktp"). The row's merchant questions were split off to row 36.

What we tried

Arm E (the usual mixture on a universe where a share of names carry their type's suffix) at marker coverage 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9 and 1.0, and a universe with markers at the front. New probes use name parts that appear in no trained name, so the marker is the only familiar piece.

Example prompt

Question: What type is Huxorc?
Answer:
  options: Huxorc is a Voltrix-type. (gold) / Huxorc is a Pyrrhan-type. / ... (8 options)

"-orc" marks Voltrix in this universe; "Hux" appears in no trained name.

How it did

Marker coverage0.30.50.70.91.0prefix 0.7
Marked name, unseen part (% right, chance 12.5)56.281.295.810010097.9
Unmarked control12.516.716.712.516.78.3
Held-out species induction, no context38.531.250.074.085.454.2

What we learned

  • The stem transfers to genuinely new names, not just recombined parts.
  • The effect rises steadily with how many trained names carry the marker, and a front marker works as well as a suffix.
  • At full coverage the model places a creature it never read about by its name alone at 85%.
  • For merchants: a family sharing a visible token (AMZN) will be grouped with its family, as reliably as the training data is consistent.

Row 22 · 2026-09-19 · REAL-1, REAL-4, GRAPH-2, GRAPH-6 · §36 Real-use replicate testdone

A realistic transaction history for a brand-new user

Why we ran it

Every merchant result so far used 120 invented names, one clean string each, balanced categories; every prototype result used tidy 3-way trials. The encoder results of row 13, the realism work of row 21 and the bank-string normaliser of row 36 fed this test on a history that looks like a real statement.

What we tried

No training. 3,000 transactions over 240 merchants (120 real chains like Kroger and Shell, 120 invented), with skewed (Zipf) frequencies, noisy statement strings, amounts and weekdays. Given k labelled transactions per category (1, 3, 10), frozen MiniLM and bge-base label the rest 12-way by: the centroid of labelled examples ("prototype"), label propagation over a nearest-neighbour graph, the category name's own embedding, or a mix of name and centroid.

Example prompt

CHECKCARD 08/21 UNITED AIRLINES DENVER CO      Travel         $590.51  Thu
POS DEBIT ELRHURST CO #6387 PORTLAND OR 07/20  Restaurants    $7.89    Sun
PP*UNITED A 6246                               Travel         $149.38  Sat
POS RGL CNMS 0353                              Entertainment  $56.80   Sat

Rows from the frozen history: the same merchant appears in several renderings, and "Elrhurst Co" is an invented merchant no model has read about.

How it did

MiniLM, normalised strings (% right, chance 8.3)k=1k=3k=10
Prototype41.753.068.8
Prototype, raw strings31.040.555.2
Category name only (no examples)38.938.938.9
Name + prototype mix48.357.270.4

What we learned

  • A prototype is a memory of the labelled merchants: 85 to 90% on those, 13 to 18% on everything else.
  • For unseen real chains, the category's name reaches the encoder's own knowledge (bge-base 52 to 56%); invented merchants stay at chance for every method.
  • Normalising the string is worth about ten points; amount and weekday features hurt; label propagation loses below ten labels.

Questions it raised

  • A fine-tuned encoder on the user's history and a fact database for unknown merchants: row 33, on the evaluation set of row 35.

Row 23 · 2026-09-19 · DATA-1 · §39 Held-out partition controldone

Make "weakness" a real second attribute

Why we ran it

In the original universe each species' weakness is a fixed rotation of its type, so "group by weakness" is "group by type". The main experiment (row 2's Needs) had claimed transfer to the weakness grouping; row 20 confirmed from the saved answers that it was the type rule.

What we tried

The same 160 species, names and other attributes, with each weakness drawn independently of type (128 of 160 change). Arm C retrained (one seed, 800 steps) and the base scored on newly frozen question sets.

Example prompt

Field guide:
Blaxent: Voltrix-type, weak to Lithor, ...
Tunarn: Voltrix-type, weak to Tidewell, ...
Falath: Voltrix-type, weak to Lithor, ...
Radsup: Voltrix-type, weak to Verdane, ...

... He labeled his Tunarn 'wumbo' and his Falath 'zorp' and his Radsup 'dabble'.
Following the same rule, how is he likely to label his Blaxent?   (gold: zorp)

All four creatures share a type, so only the weakness separates them; the card is shown here for clarity, the test runs without it too.
