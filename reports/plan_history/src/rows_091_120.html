Row 97 · 2026-09-28 · EVAL-10 · REPORT §98

User effort: how much work does each auto-file policy save?

Why we ran it

A review on 2026-09-28 noted that on REAL-7 (row 93) the model's confident errors are items the prompt cannot answer. Accuracy alone does not say what a user would experience. Row 96 had also closed the misfile line, so the question became: given these models, what policy should decide which transactions to file automatically? (A companion analysis, REPORT §97, found the final recipe's confidence drops usefully when a merchant's filings are mixed: 38% of such answers confident against 99% on consistent ones; it also corrected rows 95 and 96, whose merchant matching undercounted rows.)

CPU only, over saved scores. A new "effort" metric per transaction: auto-filed and right costs 0; auto-filed and wrong costs 5 (notice, undo, refile; 3 and 10 also tried); otherwise the top three suggestions are shown, costing 1 click if the answer is among them and 3 (a search) if not. Policies: the model alone above a calibrated 98% confidence threshold (fitted on other users); the owner's history rule (auto-file when the user's last two filings of the merchant agree); history with the model agreeing; history or model.

Row 120 · 2026-09-29 · EVAL-12 · REPORT §117

Escalating the hard third to a much larger model

Why we ran it

Row 116's best policy left about 35% of transactions to the user as suggestions. Row 100 had shown the small models' remaining misses on blind_v1 are new users and new businesses, which need world knowledge. A large model might settle what the small ones do not.

What we tried

decider-35B-A3B (Apache-2.0; a 35-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with about 3B parameters active per token), untrained, read blind_v1 zero-shot in its own layout (options after the question, letter labels) on one H100. What we learned

  • The large model knows what the small ones do not (new users, new businesses) and does not know what they were taught (idiosyncratic filings 49, changes of mind 28, weekday splits 46): it follows what the merchant is, not what this user does with it.
