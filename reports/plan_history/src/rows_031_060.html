Repairing forgetting by distilling from the model as it was before injection

Why we ran it

"Arm C" is the project's main knowledge-injection recipe: a rank-64 LoRA adapter (a small trainable add-on to the frozen weights) on Qwen2.5-3B, trained on sentences about 136 invented species plus labelling exercises. What we tried

Row 32 · 2026-09-18 · TRAIN-9 · REPORT §32

Does a LoRA adapter want ten times the learning rate?

Why we ran it

Thinking Machines' "Tinker" LoRA primer says adapters want about ten times the full fine-tuning learning rate. Every decoder adapter here trained at 1e-4, the sweep of row 17 stopped at 2e-4, and in row 18 the Flan-T5 adapter at 3e-4 learned nothing while full fine-tuning at the same rate recalled 98%. If the primer held, those verdicts were artefacts of a too-low rate.

What we tried

Four runs: arm C (Qwen2.5-3B, rank 64, alpha 128, 800 steps) at 5e-4 and 1e-3, and the Flan-T5-large knowledge-only adapter at 1e-3 and 3e-3, all scored on the same ladder of fact, reasoning and general-ability items.

Answer:
  options: " FooFoo" / " tandle" / " snerk"

An induction item: the model can only find Timmy's rule by using the species types it was taught (Dolorc shares Nolvan's type).

Users split into four folds of five; each fold's adapter trains on the other fifteen and scores its five, so all 1,179 items are read by a model that never saw that user. Row 49 · 2026-09-23 · STAT-4 · REPORT §50

Can the categoriser's confidence decide when to file automatically?

Why we ran it

A production categoriser needs a rule for when to apply a label without asking. What we learned

  • 16 sequences per step at 1e-4 for 200 steps (three minutes) is already the efficient point.
  • Bigger batches match it only with the rate scaled; batch 64 unscaled stops at 72.3. Two to four times the data per step buys at most about a point for two to four times the compute.
  • The H100 is saturated at 8 to 16 sequences per pass; larger passes are slower because of padding.

Questions it raised
