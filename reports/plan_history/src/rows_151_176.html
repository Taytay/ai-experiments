Row 151 · 2026-10-01 · MODEL-22 · §146 strands-decider, an outside model

Trying AWS's new open "decider" model, strands-decider, on our task

Why we ran it

The owner found a newly published open-source decision model from AWS (strands-decider, Apache-2.0) and asked how it handles our datasets compared with our best reader, decider-4B trained with "the recipe" (our standard set of training episodes). It has a different design: a Qwen3.5-2B-Base body with its word-predicting head thrown away, and a small (~1M-parameter) "pointer head" that scores each option by comparing the hidden state at the answer position with the hidden state at the end of each option's line.

What we tried

First we checked we were using it correctly: their engine on their README example, their own evaluation on their held-out rows, our reader against theirs on 61 questions (same answer on all 61), and an easy ladder of obvious transactions. Then we scored the published v19 checkpoint untrained, and trained it on the recipe's 12,800 episode draws three ways: continuing v19 in our loop, a fresh strands head on Qwen3.5-2B-Base in our loop, and strands' own trainer and config. decider-2B with the recipe was the same-size comparison. Each trained arm took about 22 minutes on one H100.

Row 168 · 2026-10-03 · REAL-28 · proposed

Rewarding "might be" and penalising "definitely isn't"

Why we ran it

Row 167 measured how much probability the model leaves on categories a payee is never filed under. The owner suggested rewarding the model for knowing which categories something might be versus definitely isn't, or for suggesting a category that is ever used, even in the future.

What we tried

Proposed; it needs real training data from several consenting budgets, with users held out. Add to the one-slot loss: (a) a penalty on probability given to categories the user never files that payee under; (b) partial credit for any category the user ever files it under, past or future (a training-only signal); (c) the ranked-list cost (the work-saved scale) as a differentiable expected cost; and Clef's label smoothing plus Brier loss for calibration. Judge by right first, top 3/5/10, MRR, probability on never-used categories and work saved, on held-out users and later months. No RL loop is needed: the readout gives the whole distribution in one pass.

Questions it raised

  • Waits on multi-user real data.
