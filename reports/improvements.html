Teaching a model new entities: what improved, and how to train next

RTX 3090 experiments, 12 to 14 September 2026. Merchant database (120 fictional stores) and a Pokemon-style creature universe (160 species, 8 fictional types). Every number here is from a tracked run in evals/LEADERBOARD.md. Section 5 reports the curriculum experiment that the first version of this page only proposed. Section 0 defines every term used on the page with the actual strings the models saw, and each chart is followed by the items it scores and what the bars mean.

What the four tiles mean
  • 59% vs 40%. The model is shown three creature cards with made-up tags (his Nolvan 'FooFoo', his Noline 'tandle', his Yoreel 'snerk') and asked how a fourth card (Dolorc) will be tagged. No facts are in the prompt; the model must know from training that Dolorc and Nolvan are both Lithor-type. A model trained on a mix of facts and practice episodes gets 95 of 160 such items right (59%). A model trained on the facts alone gets 64 right (40%). Guessing gets 53 (33%).
  • 78% vs 51%. The same kind of few-shot prompt, but on public text-classification data the model was never trained on, with the class names replaced by nonsense words. This measures whether training on our private data broke the model's general ability to learn a labeling rule from examples. Facts-only training broke it (60 down to 51); the mixed run improved it (78).
  • 41.7% vs 21.7%. Given Merchant: Elrholm, pick the spending category out of 12. The training text never says "Groceries"; it says Elrholm sells canned goods, packaged snacks and frozen vegetables. Fourteen varied sentences per store lets the model make that leap for 50 of 120 stores; one sentence repeated 56 times manages 26.
  • 85%. An embedding model, not a language model. A user's label is represented by the average embedding of the three items the user put under it. A new item is assigned to the nearest label. On 3-way tasks this is right 85% of the time when the user's grouping follows type, and 63% when it follows habitat, an attribute the label never names.

0. How to read this page: the datasets, the tasks, and the words used for them

Two synthetic datasets appear throughout. Both use invented names so the pretrained model can have no prior knowledge of any entity; anything above chance after training is knowledge that training put there.

Merchant database (sections 1 and 4)
120 fictional stores, 10 per spending category, across 12 categories (Groceries, Restaurants, Gas & Auto, Clothing, Electronics, Home Improvement, Pharmacy & Health, Entertainment, Travel, Pets, Fitness, Telecom & Utilities). Each store sells three products drawn from its category's pool. The category word itself never appears in any training text.
One merchant record 
name:      Elrholm
category:  Groceries          (never shown to the model)
products:  canned goods, packaged snacks, frozen vegetables
raw fact:  "Elrholm is a store that sells canned goods, packaged snacks and frozen vegetables."
bank form: "DEBIT CARD PURCHASE ELRHOLM STORE 4970 TUCSON AZ"   (never shown in training)

Other stores look like Oskhurst Bros, Orlwick Co, Gridale, Bexstead Ltd. The "bank form" is the string a bank statement would show: uppercase, a store number, a city, sometimes a processor prefix such as SQ * or POS DEBIT.

Creature universe (sections 2, 3 and 5)
160 species with opaque names and 8 invented types (Voltrix, Pyrrhan, Tidewell, Lithor, Verdane, Umbrine, Gustral, Frostel). Each species also has a weakness (a type; fixed per type, so all Frostel-types are weak to Tidewell), a habitat (cave, marsh, canopy, tundra, dune, reef), a diet, a region and an evolution stage. 24 species (3 per type) are held out: they appear in no training text of any kind. Unlike the merchants, the type label is present in training text here; the question is analogy over known attributes, not inference of hidden ones.
One species record and its field-guide entry 
name: Javish   type: Frostel   weakness: Tidewell   habitat: canopy
diet: mineralivore   region: Korrath   stage: 1   held out: no

field-guide entry (used when "facts are in context"):
"Javish: Frostel-type, weak to Tidewell, canopy habitat, mineralivore, Korrath."

Held-out species include Cynine (Lithor), Ekkeel (Voltrix), Blaxash (Gustral), Kelilk (Frostel). When the page says a result is "on held-out species", the model has never seen those names.

Knowledge text, "augmented" vs "raw"
Raw means one canonical sentence per entity (the "database dump"). Augmented means the same facts rendered many ways: paraphrases, question-and-answer pairs, a negative, and comparisons with other entities. The merchant recipe has 14 texts per store; the universe recipe has about 20 per species (2,752 texts for the 136 trained species).

How every accuracy on this page is scored, and what "chance" means
Every task is multiple choice. The model is given a prompt and a fixed set of answer strings, and the answer whose tokens it assigns the highest average log-probability wins. Nothing is generated or parsed, so a wrong answer is a wrong preference, not a formatting slip. Chance is what picking at random would score: 12-way 8.3%, 8-way 12.5%, 4-way 25%, 3-way 33.3%, yes/no 50%. Every chart draws chance as a dashed line; a bar at the line means the model learned nothing measurable for that task.

Recall, manipulation, induction
Three different things a model can do with a fact, tested separately because a model can have one without the others.

Recall: retrieve the stored attribute directly.

Question: What type is Syaott?
Answer:
options:  Syaott is a Voltrix-type. |  Syaott is a Pyrrhan-type. | ... (8)     correct: Syaott is a Verdane-type.

Manipulation: answer a question whose form was not the form the fact was stored in. Two are used: yes/no membership and pairwise comparison.

Question: Is Kelome a Verdane-type creature? Answer yes or no.
Answer:                              options:  Yes |  No      correct: No

Question: Do Lumeel and Frodux share the same type? Answer yes or no.
Answer:                              options:  Yes |  No      correct: No

Induction (the "Timmy task", also called label induction or few-shot label induction on this page): infer a labeling rule from a few examples and apply it. The labels are nonsense words, so knowing the entities is necessary but not sufficient; the model must also work out what the labels track.

Timmy labels his creature cards with his own made-up tags. He labeled his Nolvan 'FooFoo' and
his Noline 'tandle' and his Yoreel 'snerk'. Following the same rule, how is he likely to label his Dolorc?
Answer:                              options:  FooFoo |  tandle |  snerk      correct: FooFoo

Dolorc and Nolvan are both Lithor-type; Noline is Gustral and Yoreel is Tidewell. So the rule is "tag by type" and Dolorc gets Nolvan's tag. k is the number of demonstration cards: k=3 is the default, k=2 is easier (chance 50), k=4 harder (chance 25). "Real-name labels" is the control where the tags are the actual type names (his Velrock 'Gustral' and his Velott 'Pyrrhan'...), which lets the model lean on what it already knows about the words.

"From the weights" (no context) vs "with context" (facts in the prompt)
Every item is run twice. Without context, the prompt is exactly as above and the model must know the species from training; this measures parametric knowledge, "from the weights". With context, the field-guide entries of every species named in the item are prepended, which is what a retrieval (RAG) system would do; this measures whether the model can use facts it is handed.

The base 3B model scores 35.6 on this item type without context (it does not know the species) and 48.8 with context (it knows them now, and can partly do the reasoning). A well-trained model should approach the with-context number without the context.

"Trained format" vs "bare format"
The answer options can be a bare type name ( Frostel) or a full sentence in the shape the training text used ( Syaott is a Frostel-type.). Models that had learned every fact scored only about 20% with bare names and 100% with the trained sentence shape, so a bare-format number measures the prompt shape as much as the knowledge. Recall is reported in the trained format on this page, and the bare-format rows in section 5 are kept only as a control.
Symbol-tuning episode (the "episodes" training stream)
A training example that looks like the Timmy task but is generated fresh from the database: pick an attribute to group by (type, habitat, region or diet; never weakness), pick 2 to 5 groups, one or two example species per group, invent a random label per group, ask for the label of a new species. Seven sentence templates and eight narrators are used; the test's exact "Timmy labels his creature cards" wording and its ten test labels (FooFoo, blammo, zorp, quix...) never appear. Loss is computed only on the answer. Half the episodes carry the field-guide entries in the prompt. "Symbol tuning" is the name Wei et al. (2023) gave to this kind of training.

Replay (the "generic replay" stream) and the ICL regression suite
ICL is in-context learning: picking up a task from examples in the prompt. Two sets of public few-shot classification items are used and they share no dataset. Replay episodes are mixed into training (AG News, Emotion, TREC, 20 Newsgroups; five prompt formats; 80% random-symbol labels, 20% natural class names). The ICL regression suite is evaluation only (SST-2 sentiment, Banking77 intents, DBpedia-14 topics, Subj subjectivity; 48 items per dataset per label style; one fixed format; 2 to 4 classes, so chance is 43.3%). "Symbol" items replace class names with nonsense words; "natural" items keep them. The symbol score is the one that matters: it asks whether the model can still learn a rule from examples when the label words carry no meaning.

Perplexity
A forgetting gauge. The model reads a neutral passage of English (a 300-word history of coffee that has nothing to do with either dataset) and perplexity is the exponential of its average per-token loss. Roughly, a perplexity of 8.5 means the model is as unsure at each word as if it were choosing among 8.5 equally likely words; the base 3B model scores 8.5, the base 0.5B model 16. Doubling to 15 or 20 is noticeable drift. 400 to 800 means the model can no longer produce ordinary English.
Full fine-tuning, LoRA, QLoRA, learning rate
Full fine-tuning updates every weight. LoRA freezes the model and trains small low-rank adapter matrices next to each linear layer; rank 64 on all seven projection matrices gives 35M trainable parameters on the 0.5B model and 120M on 3B. It learns less and forgets less. QLoRA is LoRA on a base model stored in 4-bit, to fit larger models. The learning rate sets step size; the page uses 1e-5 and 5e-5 for full fine-tuning and 1e-4 to 3e-4 for LoRA. "gc" is gradient checkpointing, which trades compute for memory.
Prototype (embedding model)
An embedding model maps a string to a vector. A prototype for a user's label is the average of the vectors of the items the user gave that label. A new item is classified by the nearest prototype. No training happens when a user invents a label; the training that does happen (once, contrastively) is to make a species' name embed near the text of its attributes.
Example

Timmy tagged Nolvan, Dolorc and Wexash 'FooFoo' (all Lithor-type), Noline, Radorc and Zarig 'tandle' (Gustral) and Yoreel, Lumeel and Hysine 'snerk' (Tidewell). The 'FooFoo' prototype is the mean of the three name embeddings. A new card, Kroath (Lithor), is assigned to whichever of the three prototypes its own embedding is closest to. When the user's grouping follows habitat instead (all the 'zorp' cards live on reefs), the same procedure works if reef-dwellers' names cluster in the embedding space, which the training encouraged by pairing each name with "Creatures that live in reef habitats."

Subwords, new tokens, "the pieces"
A tokenizer splits text into pieces from a fixed vocabulary. Unknown words become several pieces: MiniLM's uncased WordPiece renders both "Elrholm" and "ELRHOLM" as el ##rh ##ol ##m. Adding a token means giving "Elrholm" its own single vocabulary entry with a fresh embedding row. The page's finding is that the shared pieces are what let knowledge learned from one spelling apply to another.
Morphology universe, marked and plain names, probes
A second build of the creature universe where 70% of species names end in a suffix tied to their type: -orc Voltrix, -ash Pyrrhan, -eel Tidewell, -ath Lithor, -ilk Verdane, -ome Umbrine, -urn Gustral, -ent Frostel. The other 30% use neutral suffixes. This is the universe's stand-in for drug stems (-pril means ACE inhibitor) and retailer name variants. A marked name carries its type's suffix; a plain name does not. Probes are 96 never-trained names, 48 marked and 48 plain, asked "What type is X?"

Tokens per second, GiB, MFU
Training throughput is counted in tokens processed per second. For scale, the interleaved run in section 5 processed 1.25 million training tokens and took 24.6 minutes at 851 tokens/s on the 3B model; a 512-token sequence is about 20 short fact sentences packed together, or one episode with its field guide. GiB is peak GPU memory; the card has 24. MFU (model FLOPs utilization) is the share of the GPU's arithmetic peak the training loop actually uses; 13 to 17% means the GPU is mostly waiting on Python and kernel launches, 69% means it is busy.

1. What improved, in order of impact

  1. Diverse text instead of a database dump. One sentence per entity, repeated 56 times, is memorized but barely usable (21.7% category inference). The same facts as 14 paraphrases and question-answer pairs double it (41.7%) and go from chance to 42.5% on reverse lookup. This is the single largest lever and it costs nothing at inference.
    What "memorized but barely usable" looks like

    The raw-fact model saw "Elrholm is a store that sells canned goods, packaged snacks and frozen vegetables." 56 times and reached near-zero training loss on it. Asked in a shape it never saw, Merchant: Elrholm / Spending category:, it picks Groceries for only 26 of 120 stores. Asked the reverse, Which store sells canned goods and frozen vegetables? with Elrholm among four store names, it is at chance (25.8%): it learned name-to-products and not products-to-name, the "reversal curse". The augmented model, which also saw "Question: Where can I buy packaged snacks? Answer: You can buy packaged snacks at Elrholm.", gets the reverse question right 42.5% of the time and the category 41.7%.

  2. Learning rate. Full fine-tuning at 5e-5 destroyed a 0.5B model (perplexity 16 to 800) and with it the products-to-category reasoning the task needs. At 1e-5 the same data gives the best result with perplexity 26. For LoRA, 2e-4 on rank 64 was also too hot; 1e-4 halved the damage with identical recall.
    What "destroyed" means here

    At 5e-5 the augmented model still answered What does Elrholm sell? correctly 46.7% of the time (it kept the facts) but its category inference fell to 9.2%, chance, because the step from "canned goods and frozen vegetables" to "Groceries" needs the general model that the training wrecked (perplexity 796 on the coffee passage means it can no longer predict plain English). The same data at 1e-5 gives 41.7% on category with perplexity 26. For the 3B LoRA runs in the universe, lr 2e-4 pushed perplexity from 8.6 to 31.8, lr 1e-4 to 20.5, and both recalled 100% of species types in the trained format.

  3. No new tokens. Adding entity names as vocabulary collapsed the embedding model's transfer to statement-format strings from 70.8% to 23% and cut LLM extraction from 46.7% to 31.7%. Subword pieces are what let a learned association survive a format change, and they are the only route to your "slight variation on a name still means something" goal (section 4).
    Example

    The embedding model was trained only on clean sentences like "Shoppers go to Elrholm for canned goods..." and then asked to categorize "DEBIT CARD PURCHASE ELRHOLM STORE 4970 TUCSON AZ", which it had never seen. With the stock tokenizer, the pieces el ##rh ##ol ##m are the same in both strings and 68 of 96 trained stores are categorized correctly (70.8%). With "elrholm" added as one token, that single token does appear inside 105 of the 120 bank strings, yet accuracy drops to 22 of 96 (22.9%). The one new embedding is diluted by averaging over the other 12 or so tokens in the string, and it is brittle: adding just " store 4970 tucson az" after the bare name drops the new-token model from 100% to 37.5% while the subword model holds at 93.8%.

  4. Prototypes for user-defined labels. Defining a label as the centroid of the user's own examples handles nonsense names, synonyms and latent groupings with no retraining: 85% on type and 63% on habitat from three cards, against 33% chance.
    Example

    Three cards labeled 'FooFoo', three 'tandle', three 'snerk'. Average each group's name embeddings, then place a tenth card by nearest average. The label strings are never embedded; they could be anything. If the user had instead sorted the cards by where the creatures live, the same procedure is right 63% of the time (chance 33) even though no label says "reef" or "marsh".

  5. Model scale for few-shot label induction. The Timmy prompt is solvable by a 3B model with facts in context (49 to 52%) and not by a 0.5B model under any condition. Below a few billion parameters the model cannot override its label priors from three examples.
    Example

    Give the 0.5B model the field-guide entries for Dolorc, Nolvan, Noline and Yoreel, which state plainly that Dolorc and Nolvan are both Lithor-type, and ask how Timmy tags Dolorc. It still picks among FooFoo, tandle and snerk at chance (32.5%). The 3B model with the same context gets 48.8%. Swap the nonsense tags for the real type names and both models improve (54 and 68%): the small model can follow labels that already mean something, and cannot follow labels that do not.

  6. Pipeline. Unsloth's fused kernels and loss give 2.2x throughput and 3x memory reduction at 512 tokens; sequence packing matters as much on small models, which are launch-overhead-bound at 13 to 17% GPU utilization on short facts. A 6-attribute entity costs about 1,800 training tokens for 100% recall, so 10,000 entities train in 17 minutes on 0.5B or 2 to 4 hours on 3B to 7B.
    Example

    "Javish is a Frostel-type creature. It lives in canopy habitats..." is about 25 tokens. Training on one such sentence per sequence leaves the GPU idle between tiny kernel launches; packing 20 of them into one 512-token sequence nearly doubles tokens per second on the same hardware. The 1,800-token budget is the roughly 20 texts per species, each seen about 3.5 times, that gave 100% type recall in section 5.

2. Your goals, restated against the data

You are fine with general perplexity rising, want to keep enough of the base model to bootstrap the target tasks, and want the result to be better at few-shot classification on unseen categories and inputs. Three of the findings bear directly on that.

The one kind of forgetting you cannot afford is loss of in-context learning. Every LoRA run on declarative text recalled its facts perfectly (100% in the trained format) and every one of them got worse at the few-shot label task when the facts were then supplied in context: 3B fell from 48.8 to 34.4 after training on declarative text, and on public few-shot data from 60 to 51. Perplexity on English prose is the wrong regression metric for you. The right one is a small suite of few-shot tasks with arbitrary labels, run before and after every training job; section 5 builds that suite and shows the data mix that reverses the loss.

What the loss looked like on public data

Before training, the 3B model reads the Banking77 item in section 0 (two customer messages tagged 'Snayen', two tagged 'Graax', two tagged 'Yenpel', then a new message) and picks the right nonsense tag 66.7% of the time. After 800 steps on creature facts alone (arm A in section 5) it scores 52.1% on the identical items. On DBpedia topic items it fell from 52.1 to 41.7, below the 43 chance level for the suite. Nothing about banks or encyclopedias was in the training data; the model lost the general habit of "the label follows the examples" while it was learning "Javish is a Frostel-type". Perplexity on the coffee passage rose from 8.5 to 21 in the same run, which is a real change but not the one that matters for your goal: arm Cn later reached perplexity 20 with public few-shot at 71, and arm D reached perplexity 14 with 81, so perplexity does not track the skill you care about.

Second, knowledge injected as statements did not become knowledge the model could use for analogy. Recall and manipulation are separate skills (Physics of Language Models 3.2), and our ladder shows the split: yes/no and comparison formats that appeared in training scored 85 to 94%, the never-trained Timmy format sat at chance. Third, the embedding side already does what you want for unseen categories: a label is a centroid of examples, so a synonym, a nonsense tag, or a grouping the label never names all work without retraining.

The split, on one species

The knowledge-only 3B model (arm A) answers Is Kelome a Verdane-type creature? correctly 96% of the time and Do Lumeel and Frodux share the same type? 91% of the time. Both question shapes appeared in its training text ("Question: Is Javish a Pyrrhan-type? Answer: No, ..."; "Question: Do Javish and Gorath share a type? Answer: Yes, ..."). The Timmy prompt shape did not, and the same model picks the right tag for Dolorc 40% of the time, barely above the 33 chance line. It can retrieve "Dolorc is Lithor" and "Nolvan is Lithor" separately and confirm them when asked directly; it cannot notice on its own that this is why they share a tag.

3. Inject first and then teach the task, or teach the task as the injection?

Recommendation: one interleaved curriculum, not two stages (tested in section 5)

Mix three kinds of examples in every batch of a single training run:

  1. Knowledge text about the entities, in many surface forms and both directions (the augmented recipe that already works), including statement-style noisy variants of names.
    Example
    Trainers classify Javish as Frostel-type; like all Frostel-types it is weak to Tidewell-type attacks.
Question: Name a Frostel-type creature.
Answer: Javish is a Frostel-type creature.

    Loss is taken on every token of the text.

  2. Few-shot episodes built from the same database: "A is 'zorp', B is 'quix', C is 'zorp'. D is ___", with labels drawn fresh and at random each episode, the grouping attribute varied (type, habitat, price band, region), the option set sometimes given and sometimes not, and a slice of entities and attributes held out so the task itself is tested on unseen categories. This is symbol tuning (Wei et al. 2023) applied to your own data, and it is the mechanism that teaches the model to override its label priors from examples.
    Example (grouped by region, four groups, two cards each)
    Timmy sorts creature cards into piles with made-up names. So far: Gorome = Luszelhob; Dolvan = Kizelfim;
Radble = Kizelfim; Quonash = 866; Syaoz = 866; Radurn = quodexsna; Huloz = quodexsna; Radsup = Luszelhob.
Which pile does Faline go in?
Answer: 866

    Loss is taken only on the answer token(s). The model has to know from its weights that Faline, Quonash and Syaoz share a region, since no field guide is given in this episode. For the merchant version, the "cards" are transactions and the hidden grouping might be category, merchant, or amount band.

  3. A small replay slice (10 to 20%) of generic few-shot tasks with random labels, so the in-context skill is preserved rather than merely not attacked.
    Example (TREC question types)
    Text: What is Head Start ?
Category: snagrafim

Text: Who 's the only president buried in Washington
Category: 535

Text: What does IQ stand for ?
Category: Epcu

Text: What does JESSICA mean ?
Category: Epcu

    Nothing here is about creatures or merchants. Its job is to keep "follow the labeling rule in the examples" a general habit rather than one that only fires on the creature vocabulary.

Why not sequential. Stage one (knowledge) measurably damages the skill that stage two needs, and stage two then overwrites stage one: two rounds of forgetting with nothing holding either in place. The literature is consistent on this: Physics of LMs 3.1 finds knowledge is only extractable when instruction-style data is mixed into pretraining rather than added afterwards; Instruction Pre-Training and Self-Tuning are the same idea at scale; MetaICL and symbol tuning show the few-shot skill is itself trainable and transfers to unseen tasks and labels.

What "stage two overwrites stage one" looked like when it was run (arm D, section 5)

After 400 steps of knowledge text the model presumably knew every species (arm A at 800 steps did). After 400 more steps of episodes, asked What type is Syaott? with the trained-format options, it is right 96.9% of the time instead of 100%; given the field-guide entry for Syaott in the prompt, only 89.4%, when every other arm is at 100%. Asked Is Kelome a Verdane-type creature? it scores 63.8% against the interleaved run's 85.0%. The second stage taught it to answer in the episode style, and the fact-question style it had learned in stage one drifted.

Why not episodes alone. An episode teaches "these two share a group" without much exposure to why, and knowledge needs many diverse exposures to become robust. The mix gives the model the facts and the habit of using them. Expect the episodes to be the smaller share of tokens (a quarter to a third) but the larger share of the effect on your target metric.

What "episodes alone" produced (arm B)

6,000 episodes mention every trained species many times, always as "Ulkdor = 497" style pairs, never as "Ulkdor is a Frostel-type". After 800 steps the model still cannot answer What type is Syaott? (12.5%, chance) and is at chance on the Timmy task from its weights (32.5%). But given the field guide it does the Timmy task at 78.8% (base 48.8), and it does the public Banking77 symbol items at 91.7% (base 66.7). It learned the skill perfectly and none of the facts.

In practice, with unsloth on this card: build the mixed dataset as packed 512-token sequences, train full or high-rank LoRA at 1e-4 or below since you accept general forgetting, keep the held-out attribute and held-out entity splits sacred, and let the harness compare "knowledge only", "episodes only", "interleaved", and "sequential" on the same ladder. Section 5 reports exactly that sweep, run on 3B in one evening.

4. Getting meaning from a name variant, a drug stem, or a molecule

This is a question about what the tokenizer's subword pieces have been taught. The mechanism already exists and the merchant result is the proof: fine-tuning with the stock tokenizer let "Elrholm" learned from clean sentences be recognized inside "DEBIT CARD PURCHASE ELRHOLM STORE 4970" at 70.8%, because the pieces el ##rh ##ol ##m are shared. Whole-word tokens broke that link. Held-out species stayed at chance for the opposite reason: their names carried no shared signal at all, so there was nothing to generalize from.

The two failure directions, side by side
  • Signal in the pieces, pieces replaced: "Elrholm" with its own token. The model learns "token 151936 sells groceries" and that token, alone in a sea of uppercase noise, is not enough. 70.8% falls to 22.9%.
  • Pieces intact, no signal in them: the held-out species "Cynine" (Lithor) in the plain universe. Its pieces are shared with other names, but in that universe a name's spelling is unrelated to its type, so "Cyn" and "ine" carry nothing. Asked its type, every model is at 12.5%, chance, and should be.

The morphology universe in section 5 is the case where both conditions are met: the pieces are shared and the training data makes them predictive.

So the design goal is to make sure the signal is in the pieces, and to train on the variation you want to be robust to:

  • Drug names. Real nonproprietary names carry class in their stems by construction: -mab monoclonal antibody, -pril ACE inhibitor, -statin, -olol, -prazole. A model trained on a few thousand drugs with their classes and mechanisms will attach those stems to meaning, and a new -tinib lands near the kinase inhibitors before anyone tells it. Public data: DrugBank open release, ChEMBL, RxNorm with ATC classes, the WHO INN stem list. Molecules are a separate modality; SMILES strings tokenize into fragments the same way, and the same test applies.
    The universe's version of this

    -orc is to Voltrix what -pril is to ACE inhibitors. Ulkorc, Orvorc, Briorc, Nolorc and Syaorc are trained Voltrix species; Myrorc is a never-trained probe name. Section 5 shows a model trained on that universe types Myrorc as Voltrix 94% of the time. The real-world replicate (lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril trained; a new -pril probed) has not been run yet.

  • Retailer and processor names. Train every entity under several noisy renderings: uppercase, truncated at 20 to 25 characters, with store numbers, cities, and processor prefixes (SQ *, TST*, PAYPAL *), with and without spaces. The model then learns which pieces are the identity and which are noise, and a variant it has never seen inherits the meaning of the pieces it shares. Ten thousand retailers is exactly the scale where this starts to pay off.
    The renderings the merchant experiment generates (only one per store was used, and only at test time)
    POS DEBIT ELRHOLM #4970 TUCSON AZ 08/12
CARD PURCHASE ELRHOLM 970 TUCSON AZ
ELRHOLM*04970 TUCSON AZ 08/12
CHECKCARD 08/12 ELRHOLM TUCSON AZ
DEBIT CARD PURCHASE ELRHOLM STORE 4970 TUCSON AZ
SQ *ELRHOLM TUCSON AZ 08/12

    The recommendation is to put all six (and the clean form) in the training text for every store, so the model sees that "ELRHOLM" is constant and "SQ *", the store number and the city vary. In the 0.5B experiment, where training text contained only the clean form, every method including retrieval failed on the bank form (8 to 14%, chance), so the alternative is to normalize the string before the model sees it.

  • A test that measures it. Generate species (or merchants) whose names embed a morphological marker for their type with some probability, hold them out entirely, and score type inference on the held-out set. At zero probability the answer is chance (our current result); the slope against probability is the model's morphology transfer. Add the drug set as a real-world replicate.
    The test as run in section 5

    Probability 0.7: 70% of names marked. 48 marked probes (Myrorc, ...) and 48 plain probes (Frodux, ...), none ever trained. Marked probes typed at 93.8% and plain probes at 14.6% after training on that universe, against 12.5% chance. One point on the slope; the other measured point is probability 0 (arm C), where marked probes score 4.2%.

5. Curriculum ladder v2: the experiment, run

Qwen2.5-3B, unsloth LoRA rank 64, lr 1e-4, 800 steps of 16 sequences for every arm, one evaluation for all of them: the section-6 ladder without and with facts in context, a held-out-species induction level, morphology probes, an ICL regression suite of few-shot classification on four public datasets (SST-2, Banking77, DBpedia, Subj; random-symbol and natural labels; chance 43.3), and perplexity. Episodes are symbol-tuning items generated from the creature database: 2 to 5 groups, fresh random labels every time, grouping attribute varied and weakness never used, half with the field guide in the prompt. Replay is the same kind of episode built from AG News, Emotion, TREC and 20 Newsgroups.

What one training batch contains in each arm
  • A: 16 knowledge texts ("Javish is a Frostel-type creature...", "Question: Do Javish and Gorath share a type? Answer: Yes...").
  • B: 16 episodes ("Ulkdor = 497; Ulkoz = vohob; ... Zarig = ?").
  • C: about 7 knowledge texts, 6 episodes and 2 or 3 replay episodes (TREC, AG News...), drawn at random in those proportions every step. Over the run: 5,845 knowledge texts, 5,034 episodes, 1,921 replay episodes.
  • Cn: 8 knowledge texts and 8 episodes, no replay.
  • D: for steps 1 to 400, about 14 knowledge texts and 2 replay episodes; for steps 401 to 800, about 14 episodes and 2 replay episodes. Same totals as C in a different order.
  • E: same as C, but every name comes from the morphology universe (Ulkorc, Zarash, Pexeel...).

Every arm is then scored on the identical 1,744 ladder items (with and without field guide), 192 probes and 384 public suite items, plus the coffee-passage perplexity. About 4,400 scored items per arm.

Reading the one-line results

"100% recall" means all 160 What type is Syaott? items answered correctly in the trained sentence format with no field guide. "Timmy from weights 59" means 95 of 160 his Nolvan 'FooFoo'... his Dolorc? items right with no field guide. "Held-out partition 54" means the Timmy items where the tags follow weakness instead of type, an attribute no training episode ever grouped by, at 53.8%. "Public few-shot 78" is the mean over the four suite datasets with symbol labels (Banking77 81, DBpedia 85, SST-2 77, Subj 69 for arm C). "No facts learned" for arm B means recall stayed at 12.5%, chance.

This is the mechanism behind the drug-stem and retailer-variant goal in section 4, measured. The suffix only became a feature when the training data made it predictive (arm C, where the same suffixes were meaningless, scores at chance on the same probes), and once it did, the model placed unseen creatures by name alone: Timmy from the weights 75 instead of 59, and induction over species it never saw rose from 31 to 47 with no context at all. Recall, the public few-shot score and perplexity were unchanged relative to arm C.

Why the Timmy numbers rise too

In the morphology universe a Timmy item might read his Ulkorc 'FooFoo' and his Zarash 'tandle' and his Pexeel 'snerk' ... his Nolorc? Arm E does not have to recall four facts to answer; it can see that Nolorc and Ulkorc end the same way. About 70% of names carry the cue, so this shortcut is available in most items and the score rises from 59 to 75. The "held-out species, no context" column is the purest version: never-trained cards, no field guide, and still 47% against 33 chance, because their names follow the same rule. The untrained base model already uses the cue a little when the field guide is in the prompt (k=4 at 38 versus 27 on the plain universe), so the suffix is a surface pattern a 3B model can pick up on the fly; training made it a reliable feature.

What this settles

  1. Teach the task as the injection. One interleaved run gives 100% recall plus in-weights label induction at 59 to 61% (base 36, knowledge-only 40) and transfers that induction to a partition never used in training. Sequential staging reaches the same induction number but loses 3 points of recall, 21 points of yes/no manipulation and 9 points of pairwise reasoning, and its answer format drifts enough that recall with the facts in the prompt drops to 89%.

    For the merchant database this means one training set in which "Elrholm sells canned goods..." paraphrases, "Elrholm = zorp; Oskhurst Bros = quix; ... Gridale = ?" episodes grouped by category or by product type, and TREC or AG News replay episodes are shuffled together, rather than a knowledge run followed by a task run.

  2. Symbol-tuning episodes are what protect and improve in-context learning. Declarative text alone lowered public-data few-shot accuracy by 10 points; adding episodes raised it 15 to 21 points above base. Your regression metric should be that suite, and the fix for the erosion seen in section 2 is in the data mix, not in a lower learning rate.

    Concretely: run the 384 Banking77, DBpedia, SST-2 and Subj items with nonsense labels before and after every training job and require the symbol-label mean not to fall. Arm A would have failed that check (60 to 51); arms B, C, Cn and D would have passed (71 to 81). Perplexity would not have separated them.

  3. Keep the replay slice. It does not change the universe results; it keeps the induction skill general (78 vs 71 on public data) and holds perplexity at 15 instead of 20.

    C and Cn differ only in whether 15% of each batch is TREC or AG News episodes instead of more creature data. On every creature test they are within noise of each other (Timmy 59 vs 61, recall 100 vs 100). On the public suite, C scores 78 and Cn 71: without replay the model's few-shot skill became partly "creature-shaped". About 1,900 replay episodes over the run bought that generality.

  4. Latent, low-frequency attributes are still the hard case. Habitat induction never passed 45% in any arm. Attributes shared by few entities need more exposures or an attribute-specific retrieval step.

    He labeled his Lumeel 'zorp' and his Velump 'snerk' and his Tunarn 'wumbo'. How is he likely to label his Ithent? Lumeel and Ithent both live on reefs; the others live in a marsh and a cave. Habitat is one of six values assigned independently of type, appears in only a few of each species' training texts, and does not correlate with anything else the model knows. Best arm 45% without context (D) and 74% with the field guide in the prompt (D), so the model can do the reasoning when the attribute is written down but does not reliably recall it. For a finance app the equivalent is a user who groups transactions by something rarely stated, such as which card they used.

Each trained arm: 21 to 25 minutes at 840 to 1,170 tokens/s and 8.7 GiB, plus 7 minutes of evaluation over 4,400 items. Not run yet: the same arms on 7B, a drug-name replicate with ATC classes, and the merchant database with statement-style noisy renderings.
